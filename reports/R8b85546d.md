# Build KCTech’s U.S. Slurry Business

KCTech’s U.S. slurry business could plausibly reach **about $0.3 million in 2026, $1.2 million in 2027, and $3 million in 2028 in a central planning case**, with a wide range from no meaningful sales to **$8 million in 2028** if multiple qualifications convert quickly. These are modeled estimates, not orders or company guidance: KCTech America reported **zero revenue in 2025**, and no public source confirms a U.S. fab qualification or purchase ([KCTech 2025 annual report](https://samara.kr/disclosure/20260313001174)). The U.S. CMP-slurry market is large—roughly **$0.26–$0.37 billion** on the available estimates—but those estimates differ in geography and methodology, and only part of that market fits KCTech’s products ([Fortune Business Insights, CMP Slurry Market](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/cmp-slurry-market-115384); [GII Research, Semiconductor CMP Slurry Market](https://www.giiresearch.com/report/psm2144048-semiconductor-cmp-slurry-market-size-share.html)). The principal commercial hurdle is not total demand but winning fab-specific evaluations against established, locally supported suppliers. Portland offers a useful base near Intel and other operating fabs, but the region also hosts direct slurry competitors. A small team should therefore prioritize a few technical beachheads, stage spending against customer evidence, and treat Intel as a lead prospect—not a presumed customer.

## The market is sizable, but an entrant’s share should start small

Available market estimates suggest a **roughly $0.26–$0.37 billion U.S. slurry market around 2025–26**, but the figures are not directly comparable. Fortune Business Insights estimates the U.S. market at **$370 million in 2026**; GII Research estimates North America at **12.1%** of a **$2.14 billion** global market in 2025, implying about **$259 million for North America**, not the United States alone ([Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/cmp-slurry-market-115384); [GII Research](https://www.giiresearch.com/report/psm2144048-semiconductor-cmp-slurry-market-size-share.html)). A separate commercial estimate puts the U.S. market at **$360 million in 2025** ([Straits Research, CMP Slurry Market](https://straitsresearch.com/report/cmp-slurry-market)). These estimates are best used as scale checks, not as a precise addressable-market series. They also describe slurry alone: SEMI’s larger **$3.5 billion for 2025 and $3.9 billion for 2026** covers worldwide slurry **and pads** together and should not be substituted for slurry-only U.S. demand ([SEMI Market Outlook](https://www.semi.org/sites/semi.org/files/2025-09/5%20Clark%20Tseng_Building%20the%20Future-AI%20Investment%2C%20Equipment%20%26%20Materials%20Market%20Outlook.pdf)).

KCTech’s clearest U.S. fit is its **ceria and silica CMP slurries, tungsten and poly-silicon products, and post-CMP cleaners**; its public materials describe applications across silicon nitride, poly-Si, tungsten, copper, oxide, and barrier-metal processes ([KCTech materials product page](https://www.kctech.com/page/product3.php)). The public information does not establish which individual products are qualified for U.S. production, however, and does not identify a reliable U.S. market split by chemistry or process. That makes a narrow, process-specific entry strategy more defensible than assuming KCTech can address the whole slurry market.

KCTech’s Korean business provides relevant product and operating context, but not a reliable average deal size. An analyst report reconstructed **KRW 133.3 billion** of semiconductor-material sales for 2023 and forecast **KRW 140.3 billion** for 2024 and **KRW 158.0 billion** for 2025; these figures are estimates and forecasts, not audited slurry-line disclosures ([2024 KCTech equity research report](https://github.com/ChoiInYeol/SNUSMIC-Portfolio/blob/main/data/silver/reports/markdown/2024-06-07_equity-research-%EC%BC%80%EC%9D%B4%EC%94%A8.md)). KCTech’s annual report combines equipment and materials within semiconductor-segment revenue and does not report customer-level slurry sales or contract values ([KCTech 2025 annual report](https://samara.kr/disclosure/20260313001174)). A press profile’s estimates that materials made up about **40%** of 2025 revenue and that **30%** of materials revenue came from overseas are directional, not audited deal-size data ([BusinessPost profile of KCTech CEO Yang Ho-geun](https://m.businesspost.co.kr/BP?command=mobile_view&num=442673)). Likewise, the reported **KRW 15.52 billion** of U.S. geographic revenue in 2025 covers all company products and is not U.S. slurry revenue ([KCTech 2025 annual report](https://samara.kr/disclosure/20260313001174)).

The Portland-area opportunity is concentrated but meaningful. Intel operates two Oregon manufacturing facilities, Microchip operates a 200-mm fab in Gresham, and TSMC operates an 8-inch fab in Camas; Micron’s first new Boise fab is expected to begin wafer output in **mid-2027**, with a second Idaho fab expected to begin initial output in **late 2028** ([Oregon DEQ, Intel Facilities and Permit](https://www.oregon.gov/deq/Programs/Pages/Intel.aspx); [Microchip, Oregon facility](https://www.microchip.com/en-us/about/careers/microchip-around-the-world/americas/oregon-us); [TSMC Washington](https://www.tsmc.com/static/abouttsmcwa/index.html); [Micron FY2026 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723125/000072312526000023/mu-20260903.htm)). There is no published, comparable regional wafer-start or slurry-purchase total. Applying an explicitly provisional **8–15%** share to the estimated U.S. market gives roughly **$21–$56 million** of Pacific Northwest slurry demand in 2026; a central assumption of **10%** of a **$320 million** market implies about **$32 million** ([Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/cmp-slurry-market-115384); [GII Research](https://www.giiresearch.com/report/psm2144048-semiconductor-cmp-slurry-market-size-share.html)). Oregon’s reported **15% share of the U.S. semiconductor workforce** supports the state’s importance as a semiconductor center, but does not establish a 15% share of slurry demand ([Business Oregon, Strategic Assessment of Oregon’s Semiconductor Ecosystem](https://www.oregon.gov/biz/Publications/A%20Strategic%20Assessment%20of%20Oregon%27s%20Semiconductor%20Ecosystem_Final%20Report_6_5_26.pdf)).

## Revenue depends on qualification, repeat supply, and account wins

KCTech America was established on **April 17, 2025**, and reported **zero 2025 revenue** and a **KRW 376.9 million net loss**; its filing describes the U.S. activity as R&D ([KCTech 2025 annual report](https://samara.kr/disclosure/20260313001174)). The available interim filing for the period ending **September 30, 2026** does not disclose standalone U.S.-subsidiary revenue, so the estimates from 2026 onward should not be mistaken for reported results ([KCTech 2026 Interim/Quarterly Report](https://financialfilings.com/filings/kctech-co-ltd/interim-quarterly-report/2026/76225226/)).

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2025: reported partial launch year | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| 2026: modeled first full calendar year | $0 | $0.3m | $1.0m |
| 2027: modeled second full calendar year | $0.3m | $1.2m | $4.0m |
| 2028: modeled third full calendar year | $0.8m | $3.0m | $8.0m |

The model calculates annual sales as the sum of each qualified account’s assumed mature annual sales multiplied by its ramp in that year; it excludes equipment, unqualified opportunities, and unsigned business. **No public source supports a Korean or U.S. average slurry deal size**, and the modeled account values are therefore planning assumptions, not observed benchmarks ([KCTech 2025 annual report](https://samara.kr/disclosure/20260313001174)). The low case assumes one modest non-Intel account begins recurring purchases after a delay. The central case assumes an anchor account—potentially Intel, but not presumed won—reaches an assumed **$2 million** annual run-rate at **15%** of that level in 2026 and **60%** in 2027; one additional account and higher anchor volume bring 2028 sales to **$3 million**. The high case assumes an early anchor qualification and multiple additional accounts; its **$4 million** 2027 and **$8 million** 2028 estimates depend on several sizable ramps, not just general market growth. These account values and ramp percentages are explicit model inputs, not customer-specific evidence.

As a broad market-share check, **$3 million** in 2028 is about **0.8%** of the cited **$360 million** 2025 U.S. market, while **$8 million** is about **2.2%**; the denominator is approximate and the comparison is not a forecast of actual share ([Straits Research](https://straitsresearch.com/report/cmp-slurry-market)). A competitor-based planning range suggests a new supplier might remain below **0.1%** in its first commercial year, reach roughly **0.1–0.5%** by years two to three, and potentially reach **0.5–1.5%** by years four to five if it secures multiple qualifications and repeat supply ([TECHCET CMP Consumables announcement](https://www.semiconductorpackagingnews.com/uploads/1/Final_CMP_PR_050124SB.pdf); [SEC filing: CMP Slurry Qualification and Cost of Ownership](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/vprr/0500/05002558.pdf)). On that basis, the central 2028 case is plausible but requires successful account conversion; the high case exceeds the conservative share range and should be treated as upside. A qualification delay can shift a substantial portion of a customer’s ramp into a later year.

## Local incumbents make technical differentiation essential

KCTech would enter an established market. The competitor set includes Fujifilm, Entegris, Qnity, Fujimi, Merck KGaA’s Versum business, Resonac, and AGC. Market-share estimates differ by source and definition: TECHCET reports Fujifilm at **31%** of the slurry market, while Fujifilm’s investor presentation reports **21%** overall and **46%** shares in copper bulk and copper barrier slurry. These should be treated as separate estimates, not combined into a single consensus figure ([TECHCET CMP Consumables announcement](https://www.semiconductorpackagingnews.com/uploads/1/Final_CMP_PR_050124SB.pdf); [Fujifilm investor presentation](https://ir.fujifilm.com/en/investors/ir-materials/presentations/session/main/0118/teaserItems1/0/tableContents/01112/multiFileUpload2_1/link/ff_presentation_20250421_002e.pdf)). Fujimi reports a **13%** share of total CMP-process slurry and a much higher share in selected FEOL categories, but its product definitions are not directly comparable with the other figures ([Fujimi Integrated Report 2024](https://www.ircms.jp/irexport/fujimiinc/file/a79408144523422.pdf)).

The most immediate regional rivals are Fujimi, with facilities and CMP-materials R&D in Tualatin and Wilsonville, and AGC, which manufactures ceria CMP slurry in Hillsboro and offers local technical support ([Fujimi: About](https://www.fujimi.com/about/); [Fujimi: R&D Chemist](https://www.fujimi.com/careers/research-development-chemist-2/); [AGC Electronics America: CMP Slurry](https://www.agcem.com/products/cmp-slurry/)). Fujifilm has a U.S. CMP-slurry production and R&D site in Arizona and announced an **$18 million** Mesa expansion scheduled to begin operating in **2027** ([Fujifilm: U.S. CMP Slurry Expansion](https://www.fujifilm.com/tw/en/news/hq/14044)). Entegris has CMP-materials design and manufacturing in Aurora, Illinois, while Qnity offers dielectric, tungsten, TSV, and advanced-packaging slurries; Merck also markets advanced oxide and ceria-based slurries with U.S. manufacturing and technical activity ([Entegris Aurora CMP Materials Certificate](https://www.entegris.com/content/dam/web/about-us/corporate-overview/documents/certificates/iso-90012015-aurora.pdf); [Qnity CMP Slurries](https://www.qnityelectronics.com/cmp-slurries.html); [Merck Advanced Oxide CMP Slurries](https://www.emdgroup.com/en/expertise/semiconductors/offering/advanced-oxide-cmp-slurries.html)). There is no reliable public ranking of U.S.- or Pacific Northwest-specific supplier shares.

Prioritize Intel Oregon, Microchip and onsemi in Gresham, and other nearby operating fabs for practical technical engagement; pursue Intel Arizona, TSMC Arizona, Samsung Taylor, GlobalFoundries, Micron, TI, and SkyWater as additional prospects with differing process fit and ramp timing. These are prospects, not confirmed customers. Intel’s published supplier guidance describes corporate commodity teams involving purchasing, quality, material control, and engineering, and directs potential suppliers to its Supplier Intake Tool; TSMC, GF, Micron, Samsung, and TI also describe formal supplier or onboarding routes ([Intel Information for Potential Suppliers](https://www.intel.com/content/dam/www/central-libraries/us/en/documents/information-for-potential-suppliers.pdf); [TSMC supplier management](https://www.tsmc.com/english/aboutTSMC/supplier-management); [GF Supplier Handbook 2026](https://gf.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/GF-Supplier-Handbook-2026.pdf); [Micron suppliers](https://www.micron.com/about/company/suppliers); [Samsung sustainable supply chain](https://www.samsung.com/us/sustainability/sustainable-supply-chain/); [TI Suppliers](https://www.ti.com/en-us/about-ti/suppliers)). Dr. Lee’s publicly documented GlobalFoundries CMP work and SMART Pad co-founder role support relevant technical familiarity, but the reviewed sources do not independently verify his claimed Intel, Coherent, or Draper employment or establish buyer access ([NCCAVS CMPUG 2018 agenda](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/CMPUG2018/CMPUG418-Final-Agenda.pdf); [UC Berkeley IPIRA: SMART Pad LLC](https://ipira.berkeley.edu/smart-pad-llc); [Albany Business Review](https://www.bizjournals.com/albany/news/2021/02/25/smart-pad-semiconductor-product-commercialization.html)).

## Stage spending around evaluations and supply readiness

The conventional route is a qualification-led account process: identify a specific customer process need, find a technical sponsor and relevant sourcing stakeholders, agree on the evaluation protocol and acceptance criteria, then provide a traceable sample with specifications, lot information, safety data, handling instructions, and a named technical contact. A historical SEC filing notes that slurry qualification commonly takes **six months or more**, and that customers assess throughput and yield as well as material cost ([SEC filing: CMP Slurry Qualification and Cost of Ownership](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/vprr/0500/05002558.pdf)). A sample shipped without an agreed evaluation owner, test plan, and review date is weak evidence of progress. Supplier registration is necessary at some accounts but does not itself mean a product is approved or under evaluation.

Less conventional but practical approaches can help the team earn trust without building a full laboratory. Offer narrowly scoped co-development or second-source trials, with agreed stop/go criteria, and use external facilities for selected characterization questions rather than treating third-party testing as a substitute for fab qualification. Oregon State University’s external-user program lists a **$510** orientation fee and **$400 per month per external researcher**, while University of Oregon’s published rates include commercial FIB-SEM at **$412.69 per hour**; these are reference prices, not complete qualification budgets ([OSU MaSC: Become a user](https://research.engr.oregonstate.edu/masc/become-user); [CAMCOR FY2027 rates](https://camcor.uoregon.edu/index.php/rates-and-services/rates)). Use technical events such as SEMICON West for prearranged engineering and sourcing meetings; SEMI reported more than **35,000 attendees** and **734 exhibiting companies** for its 2026 event, but a booth is not a substitute for customer-defined trials ([SEMI on SEMICON West 2026](https://www.semi.org/en/semi-press-release/semicon-west-2026-to-spotlight-1-trillion-dollars-semiconductor-milestone-and-technologies-powering-the-industrys-next-era)). Dr. Lee’s professional connections should be used for permission-based, role-relevant introductions—not as presumed endorsements or access to confidential information.

For a four-person team using outsourced testing rather than in-house slurry manufacturing or a cleanroom, a planning allowance is **about $1.3–$2.0 million in recurring annual cash costs**, plus **$0.25–$0.65 million** for one-time setup and initial inventory. These are management estimates, not vendor quotes; the recurring range includes staffing, facilities, testing, samples and shipping, travel, compliance, insurance, and administration. Portland-area wage and industrial-rent benchmarks inform the estimate, but actual compensation and facility costs depend on the hires and site selected ([O*NET/BLS Portland sales-engineer wages](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/41-9031.00?st=OR); [O*NET/BLS Portland materials-scientist wages](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/19-2032.00?st=OR); [Kidder Mathews Portland Industrial Market Report, Q3 2026](https://kidder.com/market-reports/portland-industrial-market-report/)). This cost base makes the central revenue path a substantial investment; revenue alone cannot establish break-even because KCTech’s U.S. product margins are not public.

Before importing or storing chemicals, confirm product-by-product TSCA import requirements, SDS and labeling, hazardous-material shipping training, local fire and storage rules, and waste obligations; the applicable requirements depend on formulations and quantities ([EPA: TSCA Requirements for Importing Chemicals](https://www.epa.gov/tsca-import-export-requirements/tsca-requirements-importing-chemicals); [OSHA Hazard Communication](https://www.osha.gov/hazcom); [PHMSA Hazardous Materials Training Requirements](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/training/hazmat/training-requirements-industry); [Oregon State Fire Marshal: Hazardous Substance Storage Reporting](https://www.oregon.gov/osfm/pages/hazardous-substance-storage-reporting.aspx)). Keep initial storage and inventory modest, establish lot traceability and change control with Korean manufacturing, and expand only when customers have accepted evaluation plans and supply lead times justify local stock.

## Conclusion

The branch should be managed as a staged technical-sales investment, with its near-term value measured by customer-approved evaluations, technical progress, supplier-readiness milestones, and credible routes to repeat orders—not by the size of the national market or the number of samples shipped. A small, well-supported beachhead in one or two process niches can create a path to broader U.S. sales; a broad launch without customer-defined qualification work risks consuming the team’s time and budget before recurring demand appears.

Portland proximity is useful, but it is not a moat when local competitors already offer slurry manufacturing, R&D, or support. Preserve flexibility with outsourced testing and limited inventory, diversify the prospect pipeline beyond Intel, and make larger spending contingent on demonstrated customer progress.
