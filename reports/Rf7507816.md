# Estimate KCTech's U.S. slurry branch revenue

KCTech's Oregon branch should book roughly **$0.5–1M in 2026, $3–4M in 2027, $8–9M in 2028 and $15–17M in 2029**, with a defensible band of $2.5M (one customer, import-only) to $35M (four to six qualified fabs plus local blending) by the fourth year. The estimate is small against KCTech's Korean slurry franchise, which is worth roughly **KRW 138bn (≈US$99M)** on a broker-estimated 40% slurry share of FY2025 semiconductor revenue of KRW 345.7bn ([Wantstats](https://www.wantstats.com/company-profile/kc-tech)), and it is capped near-term by KCTech's existing total U.S. sales of **KRW 2.93bn (1.9% of revenue) in Q1 2026** ([The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=10433)). What decides the number is not market size but the **12–24 month qualification window** ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/de/news-release/2026/11/20/3407625/28124/en/global-semiconductor-cmp-slurries-and-pads-market-size-share-and-trends-forecast-to-2036-advanced-nodes-3d-nand-and-hybrid-bonding-drive-8-4-cagr-and-a-usd-8-38-billion-revenue-opp.html)) and whether two or three named fabs — Intel above all — adopt a KCTech slurry as a second source. The client's premise that KCTech's equipment business is not mature enough for the U.S. does not hold: equipment is roughly **60% of semiconductor revenue** by broker estimate ([Wantstats](https://www.wantstats.com/company-profile/kc-tech)), the "Ventus" CMP tool is being pitched to U.S. and Japanese customers ([The Guru](https://theguru.co.kr/news/article.html?no=90230)), and KCTech was selected as sole supplier of CMP equipment *and* slurry to Absolics' glass-substrate line in Covington, Georgia, with first shipments from Q4 2026 ([The Elec](https://www.thelec.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=61274)). A slurry-led U.S. entry is nevertheless the company's own stated rationale ([The Guru](https://theguru.co.kr/news/article.html?no=90230)).

## The U.S. buys $0.7–1.0 billion of CMP slurry a year, and five fabs take three-quarters of it

CMP slurry is the abrasive suspension — ceria, silica or alumina chemistry — dispensed during chemical mechanical planarization to level oxide/dielectric (STI/ILD), tungsten, copper/barrier and poly-silicon layers ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/united-states-cmp-slurries-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)). It is measured by market value, and by per-wafer consumption published per polish step — **0.5–1.2 litres per 300mm wafer per step**, with sub-7nm logic and 3D NAND consuming **25% more per wafer** than conventional designs ([24chemicalresearch](https://24chemicalresearch.com)) — not per full process flow. "Branch revenue" here means sales booked by KCTech America; no source states whether U.S. customer sales are invoiced locally or by the Korean parent ([KOSAI](https://kosai.kr/en/stock/281820.html)).

Market size is contested by a factor of three. Global Industry Analysts puts the global market at **US$2.7bn in 2024 (US$3.9bn by 2030, 6.4% CAGR) and the U.S. at US$698.0M** ([GII](https://www.gii.tw/report/go1655508-cmp-slurry.html)), consistent with another publisher's estimate that North America is **~18% of global** ([inwwin.com.cn](https://m.inwwin.com.cn)), while IndexBox puts the U.S. alone at **US$1.8–2.2bn in 2026** rising to US$3.5–4.0bn by 2035 ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/united-states-cmp-slurries-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)). This report follows the GIA basis — a **US$0.7–1.0bn U.S. market in 2025–26**, growing to ≈US$895M by 2028 — because IndexBox's figure implies the U.S. is roughly two-thirds of a global market that four other publishers size at US$2.2–2.9bn, and because a bottom-up check (38.4m U.S. wafers/yr × US$28.5 unit CMP cost) lands at **≈US$1.1bn** ([inwwin.com.cn](https://m.inwwin.com.cn)); IndexBox is carried only as a high-case sensitivity. A third estimate derived by scaling global value to the Americas' ~9% of 300mm capacity gives just **$250–350M** ([Colliers](https://www.colliers.com)); it fails for the same reason, because value share exceeds capacity share at leading-edge nodes ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/united-states-cmp-slurries-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)).

Demand is concentrated: the U.S. runs about **3.2 million wafers per month (~10% of global capacity)** ([DEV Community](https://dev.to)), and Intel, Micron, Samsung Austin/Taylor, TSMC Arizona and GlobalFoundries are estimated to account for **75–80% of U.S. slurry consumption** ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/united-states-cmp-slurries-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)). Fab-level spend is never disclosed; the estimates below combine published capacity with a derived **US$55–115 per wafer** for leading-edge logic, US$30–50 for memory and US$8–15 for mature analog, anchored on JEEZ's benchmark of **>US$50M a year at large advanced-node fabs** ([JEEZ](https://jeez-semicon.com/ja/blog/oxide-cmp-slurry-recycling-sustainability-fab-implementation-guide/)) and Caladan's **US$80K–300K per CMP tool per year** ([Caladan](https://caladansemi.com/blog/cmp-polishing-pad-slurry-consumables-guide-2026)).

| Fab | Capacity assumption | Estimated slurry spend (US$M/yr) |
|---|---|---|
| Intel Fab 52, Arizona (18A) | ~40,000 wpm ([LavX](https://news.lavx.hu)) | 40–50 |
| Intel D1X, Hillsboro OR | ~15,000 wpm ([TechPowerUp](https://www.techpowerup.com/)) | 15–20 |
| TSMC Arizona Fab 21 | 40,000–50,000 wpm ([LongPort](https://longportapp.com/)) | 40–60 |
| Samsung Taylor, TX | 20,000 wpm now, 50,000 target ([SemiconductorX](https://semiconductorx.com); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com)) | 20–60 |
| Micron Boise ID1 | first DRAM mid-2027 ([Micron](https://www.micron.com)) | 12–18 (2027) → 40–50 |
| GlobalFoundries Malta, NY | 60,000 wpm 12/14nm ([SemiconductorX](https://semiconductorx.com/)) | 8–22 |
| TI Sherman SM1 | 65–130nm, output from Dec 2025 ([TI](https://www.ti.com/)) | 4–8 |
| Specialty: glass substrate, SiC, GaN | 5,000–20,000 wpm | 1–10 each |

Capacity figures conflict materially — Samsung Taylor is reported at both 20,000 and 50,000 wpm, and TSMC Arizona at 20,000, 24,000, 40,000+ and 90,000–100,000 wpm ([ExtremeTech](https://www.extremetech.com/); [EET-China](https://www.eet-china.com/)) — so treat fab spend as ±50%. Value is not evenly spread: metal slurries hold **45–50% of U.S. value**, oxide slurries face **1–2%/yr price erosion**, and specialty/GAA chemistries grow fastest at 10–12% CAGR ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/united-states-cmp-slurries-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)). KCTech's ceria/STI strength therefore sits in the price-eroding half of the market, while its tungsten and poly-Si products meet entrenched incumbents.

## Five incumbents hold two-thirds of the market, and all of them already make slurry in the U.S.

The global slurry market is roughly **US$2.2–2.9bn in 2025–26** depending on publisher and chemistry scope ([MarketResearch.com](https://www.marketresearch.com); [Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com); [IIM](https://m.iim.net.cn/)). Entegris/CMC Materials, Fujifilm, Fujimi, Merck/Versum and DuPont are the top five at roughly **65% of slurry sales and ~77% of copper slurry** ([MarketPublishers](https://marketpublishers.com); [Gelonghui](https://dxpress.gelonghui.com)), with Entegris at **~23% global share** ([ReqoData](https://reqodata.com)) — although one source calls DuPont the largest supplier with Entegris second ([SemiconductorX](https://semiconductorx.com)), and TECHCET is reported putting Fujifilm at ~31% ([TECHCET](https://www.semiconductorpackagingnews.com/uploads/1/Final_CMP_PR_050124SB.pdf)). No U.S.-only share table exists for any supplier, including KCTech.

What matters more for a new entrant is that **every incumbent already manufactures or blends in the U.S.**: DuPont at Newark, Delaware, including a 385,000 sq ft, US$50M expansion ([Choose Delaware](https://staging.choosedelaware.com)); Entegris at Aurora, Illinois, plus US$700M of new U.S. capacity including a Colorado Springs plant ([Semiconductor Digest](https://semiconductor-digest.com/entegris-announces-plans-for-700-million-investment-in-the-united-states)); Fujifilm at Mesa, Arizona, with an **US$18M expansion starting up in 2027** ([Fujifilm](https://www.fujifilm.com/is/en/news/hq/14044)); Merck/Versum at Tempe, Arizona ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versum_Materials)); and Fujimi at Tualatin, Oregon — in KCTech's own metro — where it has supplied Intel and won Intel's supplier quality award ([Fujimi](http://www.fujimiinc.co.jp/english/ir/pdf/040323j.pdf)). Korea's Soulbrain won an **US$11.6M Texas grant** for a Taylor plant but construction stalled at land acquisition on cost and demand uncertainty ([gov.texas.gov](https://gov.texas.gov); [The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=6685)); no U.S. site exists for KCTech or Ace Nanochem. Import-only supply is therefore the only major supplier profile without U.S. assets — a point that will surface in customer audits.

Qualification is the real barrier, and the sources disagree on how long it takes. One set puts U.S. fab supplier qualification at **12–24 months overall, with second-source CMP slurry at 3–6 months and oxide slurry at 6–12 months** ([mp.weixin.qq.com](https://mp.weixin.qq.com); [inwwin](http://m.inwwin.com.cn/105/view-1211945-1.html)), while another puts new-supplier qualification at **2–4 years at a leading-edge fab**, with 18–24 months for standard slurries and 24–36 months for critical-node, multi-fab integration ([JEEZ](https://jeez-semicon.com/de/blog/Top-CMP-Materials-Suppliers-2025-Comparison/); [Forced Alpha](https://forcedalpha.com/)). This report follows the longer timeline for a first-time formulation and treats 3–6 months as the like-for-like duplicate-source case, because the short figures describe copying an already-qualified chemistry, and GlobeNewswire's "up to two years" for approval at a leading chipmaker corroborates the middle of the range ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/de/news-release/2026/11/20/3407625/28124/en/global-semiconductor-cmp-slurries-and-pads-market-size-share-and-trends-forecast-to-2036-advanced-nodes-3d-nand-and-hybrid-bonding-drive-8-4-cagr-and-a-usd-8-38-billion-revenue-opp.html)). Tier-1 fabs keep **3–5 qualified slurry suppliers per application** and qualified products typically stay for the life of the process ([JEEZ](https://jeez-semicon.com/de/blog/Top-CMP-Materials-Suppliers-2025-Comparison/)).

Litigation risk is live: the ITC found a Section 337 violation by DuPont on Cabot/Entegris' US 9,499,721 in 2021–22 and its exclusion order still bars imports of Optiplane 2300/2600 slurries after a 2024 settlement dismissed the district-court claims ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/document.ashx?g=38c8413c-2f28-4c55-93fc-766e52ff363d); [Entegris](https://www.entegris.com/content/dam/web/about-us/news/documents/entegris-settles-patent-dispute-with-dupont-de-nemours-news-release.pdf)). KCTech itself was sued in the U.S. by Hitachi Chemical over ceria slurry patents in 2011, and the suits were withdrawn in April 2013 ([JETRO](https://www.jetro.go.jp/world/asia/kr/ip/ipnews/2013/e2511aaa9ab7d1f2.html)).

## KCTech's Korean slurry franchise is worth about $50–100M and rides on two customers

KCTech reports only two segments, Semiconductor (equipment, wet cleaning and slurry bundled) and Display; FY2025 revenue was **KRW 382.9bn with a 15.7% operating margin**, up from 12.9% in 2024 on flat sales ([KOSAI](https://kosai.kr/en/stock/281820.html)). Gross margin has held at **31.0–34.7%** through the 2023 downturn, which the sources credit to recurring slurry ([Wantstats](https://www.wantstats.com/company-profile/kc-tech)). Samsung Electronics and SK hynix generated **KRW 333.66bn, or 87% of 2025 revenue** ([Vomreport](https://vomreport.com/company/281820)), implying roughly **KRW 60bn (≈US$43M) of slurry per major customer per year** — weighted toward Samsung by joint-project count (25 versus 15) ([Wantstats](https://www.wantstats.com/company-profile/kc-tech)).

That per-customer figure is the single most useful benchmark for Oregon, but the company-level total is disputed: the 40% broker split implies ~KRW 138bn of 2025 slurry revenue ([Wantstats](https://www.wantstats.com/company-profile/kc-tech)), whereas a Mirae Asset estimate puts 2025 slurry at **KRW 66.1bn against KRW 31.9bn of CMP equipment** ([Newstomato](http://www4.newstomato.com/ReadNews.aspx?no=655842)) — roughly half. The revenue model uses the lower figure as its conservative Korean benchmark and flags the higher as an alternative. Two cautions follow. First, every disclosed single supply contract is a **tool order**, not slurry — KRW 69.8bn and KRW 69.90bn from SK hynix, KRW 40.16bn (10.49% of revenue) and KRW 22.68bn from Samsung ([KOSAI](https://kosai.kr/en/stock/281820.html); [East Asia Brief](https://eastasiabrief.com/)) — so using them as slurry deal sizes would overstate a slurry contract by an order of magnitude. Second, post-qualification revenue tracks utilisation, not a ramp curve: Soulbrain's CMP slurry revenue fell **19% QoQ** (KRW 17.2bn to 13.9bn) when Samsung cut wafer input ([Asiae](https://www.asiae.co.kr); [Chosun Biz](https://biz.chosun.com)). KCTech holds an estimated **"40%+" of the Korean slurry market** ([Growth Stock Insight](https://growthstockinsight.com/cmp-슬러리-성장주-계속-오를까-첨단-공정-물량-822e0db3/)), and Samsung recognised it in April 2026 for replacing imported ceria slurry ([Wantstats](https://www.wantstats.com/company-profile/kc-tech)).

## The branch should book about $0.5M in 2026, $4M in 2027, $9M in 2028 and $15M in 2029

The model runs fab spend × qualification-driven share × addressable scope. Shares ramp at **0–2% of a target fab's slurry spend in year one, 3–8% in year two, 8–15% in year three and 15–25% in years four to five**, because a new entrant starts below the 20–33% equal split among three to five qualified suppliers and above zero only after approval ([JEEZ](https://jeez-semicon.com/de/blog/Top-CMP-Materials-Suppliers-2025-Comparison/)). Addressable scope is **30–40%** of a leading-edge fab, since ceria/silica dielectric, tungsten and poly-Si are in supply while copper/barrier remains in development ([Wantstats](https://www.wantstats.com/company-profile/kc-tech)), giving combined penetration of about 6% in year one, 12% in year two and 17.5% in year three. At a 40,000 wpm fab spending ~US$48M a year, **each percentage point of share is worth about US$0.48M** and each additional large fab at 15% adds roughly **US$7M a year**.

| Year | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 (from late 2025) | 0.1–0.2 | 0.5–1.0 | 2–3 |
| 2027 | 1.0–1.5 | 3–4 | 8–10 |
| 2028 | 3–4 | 8–9 | 18–20 |
| 2029 | 6–7 | 15–17 | 30–35 |
| 2030 | 3.5* | 22 | 40–45 |

The 2029 base-case build is Intel Fab 52 at 15% of ~US$48M (US$7.2M) + Intel Hillsboro at 10% of ~US$18M (US$1.8M) + Samsung Taylor at 8% of ~US$55M (US$4.4M) + Micron Boise at 3% of ~US$45M (US$1.35M) + TI/GlobalFoundries at 8% of ~US$10M (US$0.8M) = **US$15.5M** ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/united-states-cmp-slurries-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/); [JEEZ](https://jeez-semicon.com/ja/blog/oxide-cmp-slurry-recycling-sustainability-fab-implementation-guide/)). The high case adds TSMC Arizona at 12% and lifts Intel Fab 52 to 25%, totalling **US$35–38M**. As a sanity check, US$15M equals **1.9% of a US$790M U.S. market** and US$35M equals 4.4% — consistent with winning a handful of accounts, not national share.

Three caveats bound the estimate. It assumes U.S. sales are **booked at the branch**; because KCTech America is described as an R&D and technical-support subsidiary ([Silicon Nexus](https://siliconnexus.cloud/disclosure/q1-2026-quarterly-report-000773)), some U.S.-customer revenue may stay invoiced in Korea, so branch-booked revenue could understate U.S. sales by US$5–10M in later years. It excludes copper/barrier wins, which would expand addressable spend by 30–40%. And it rests on a customer list that is a probability-weighted target set, not signed programmes — no source shows an Intel slurry supply contract, only co-development and joint R&D ([The Bell](https://www.thebell.co.kr/front/newsview.asp?code=00&key=202510231531579400106938)). The low case assumes one late Intel qualification and import-only supply; the high case assumes an early second-source win on an already-qualified ceria chemistry, a Taylor win leveraging Samsung's Korean qualification, and a local blend line.

## Costs of $1.3–1.8M a year, a 15% tariff and a 24-month clock shape the plan

A four-person team — CEO, one sales/applications engineer, two researchers — costs about **US$1.3–1.8M a year** fully loaded: roughly US$810K of salary and benefits benchmarked to Oregon semiconductor process-engineer pay of **US$97,289–115,482** ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com); [WageFile](https://wagefiled.com)) and Intel Hillsboro CMP module roles posted at **US$133,800–255,200** ([Seedtable](https://seedtable.com)); US$50–70K for 3,000–5,000 sq ft of Portland industrial space at **US$0.62–1.20/sq ft/month** ([Colliers](https://www.colliers.com); [CommercialSearch](https://www.commercialsearch.com)); US$50–100K for third-party hazmat storage ([WSI](https://www.wsinc.com)); and US$150–400K of outsourced wafer testing. Add qualification spend of **US$50–150K per application** plus US$15–100K of test wafers ([Caladan](https://caladansemi.com/blog/cmp-polishing-pad-slurry-consumables-guide-2026)) and the branch reaches **US$2.5–4M a year** if a lab is built in-house. At a 28–35% gross margin, break-even needs US$4–6M of revenue — **2028–29**, after US$3–6M of cumulative losses.

Tariffs and logistics change the arithmetic. Korean-origin slurry entered from **14 November 2025** pays a combined **15% ad valorem** reported on heading 9903.02.80 with zero duty on the Chapter 1–97 line, so KORUS's "Free" rate for heading 3824.99 does not produce duty-free entry in practice ([CBP CSMS #67045953](http://content.govdelivery.com/bulletins/gd/USDHSCBP-3ff0a41); [FreightAmigo](https://www.freightamigo.com)); at US$80–120/gal standard oxide slurry that is **US$12–18/gal (US$3.17–4.76/L)**, and at US$180–250/gal for sub-7nm grades **US$27–37.50/gal (US$7.13–9.91/L)** ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/northern-america-silicon-oxide-slurry-for-core-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)). One set of evidence instead has Korean and Japanese slurry entering duty-free under FTAs at 2.5–5.0% MFN ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/united-states-cmp-slurries-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)); the CBP bulletin is the primary document and the report follows it. No Section 232 action covering CMP slurry was found — the January 2026 proclamation hits advanced computing chips and semiconductor manufacturing equipment at 25% ([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov)) — but the U.S.–Korea fact sheet promises Korea "no less favorable" future semiconductor treatment ([KBS World](https://world.kbs.co.kr/service/news_view.htm?lang=e&Seq_Code=197412)) and a 100% semiconductor tariff was signalled in November 2026 ([Inside Trade](https://insidetrade.com)). Ocean freight of **US$6,200–13,000 per 40ft** from Busan to Los Angeles adds only 2–3% to landed cost ([Warp](https://www.wearewarp.com)), so the tariff and the 4–6 week replenishment cycle, not freight, are the import penalty.

Oregon supply options differ sharply. Import plus local QC and warehousing fits the CEO's description of an office enabling "sample testing, customer validation and product distribution" immediately ([The Guru](https://theguru.co.kr/news/article.html?no=90230)) and needs only fire-marshal approval for corrosive/oxidizer storage. A blend plant needs an air contaminant discharge permit with a 60-day minimum application, **3–6 months typical and reportedly >18 months in DEQ's backlog** ([OpenEI](https://openei.org); [HB 4102](https://apps.oregonlegislature.gov)), a land-use compatibility statement and, if waste is treated on site, a DEQ hazardous-waste permit ([Oregon DEQ](https://www.oregon.gov/deq/hazards-and-cleanup/hw/Pages/Hazardous-Waste-Permitting.aspx)) — making **2028** the realistic first local production, or **2027** via an existing contract blender such as Kanto, High Purity Products or TOK America, all in the Portland area ([Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com)). KCTech is still only "coordinating the timing of plant construction" ([The Bell](https://www.thebell.co.kr/front/newsview.asp?code=00&key=202510231531579400106938)).

**Conventional acquisition routes** start with the newly appointed branch head's network: a SEMICON Korea speaker bio corroborates Sunghoon Lee's 15+ years in CMP R&D at GlobalFoundries and Intel and a UC Berkeley PhD ([SEMICON Korea](https://semiconkorea.org/en/node/6371)), though no public source confirms his KCTech appointment or his Coherent and Draper roles, so those rest on client information. Use that network to embed an applications engineer with an Intel CMP module team, then follow the documented path: certificates of analysis, blanket-wafer screening on the customer's own tool and pad (20–50 wafers, 2–4 weeks), patterned-wafer evaluation (4–8 weeks), then product-wafer qualification (8–20 weeks, 200–500 wafers) ([JEEZ](https://jeez-semicon.com/blog/How-to-Qualify-a-New-CMP-Slurry-Supplier/)). Hire from the pool Intel released — **~2,400 roles across four Oregon campuses from July 2026, with 529 permanent Oregon layoffs** ([BlueLine](https://bluelinesearch.com); [KGW](https://www.kgw.com)) — and lead with the Samsung 2026 award as third-party validation, targeting non-critical steps and mature nodes first, where qualification is months rather than years.

**Unexpected routes** matter more here, because the clock is the constraint. Target greenfield fabs before their process of record freezes — Samsung Taylor Fab 1 and Fab 2, TSMC Arizona, Micron Boise ID1, GlobalFoundries Malta, TI Sherman — where a new supplier can be designed in rather than substituted ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com); [GlobalFoundries](https://gf.com)). Offer slurry reclaim as a service: at >US$50M a year of spend, reclaim programmes with 80–90% material recovery become economically compelling and open sustainability conversations ([JEEZ](https://jeez-semicon.com/ja/blog/oxide-cmp-slurry-recycling-sustainability-fab-implementation-guide/)). Buy or partner with a small U.S. formulator, following Entegris's **~US$75M cash acquisition of Sinmat** ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1101302/000110130220000003/entgform8-kprojectsimba.htm)). Use a Portland-area toll blender to claim "Made in the USA" and erase the 15% duty without capex. Co-develop against a specific defect or advanced-packaging problem, the Fujifilm Mesa and Resonac US-JOINT model ([First CVC](https://en.firstcvc.jp)). Characterise KCTech slurry on KCTech's own Ventus tool during a customer's tool evaluation — the only Korean supplier with both tools and slurry can do this ([KOSAI](https://kosai.kr/en/stock/281820.html)). And pursue SiC, GaN and photonics fabs, including the Absolics glass-substrate line KCTech has already won ([The Elec](https://www.thelec.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=61274)), where incumbents are least entrenched and Dr. Lee's Coherent background is most relevant.

The risks are specific and mostly non-commercial. Qualification can slip to 2029–30 at leading-edge nodes, exhausting capital before revenue. Intel is simultaneously the anchor and the most unstable customer, cutting Oregon staff while expanding Arizona. Incumbents can bundle slurry with pads and cleans, and one is already in Tualatin. An ITC exclusion order could remove imported product from the market mid-qualification, as it did for DuPont. Customer audits may reject a supplier with no U.S. plant, and TSCA premanufacture notification for novel additives, DOT hazmat classification (5–10% logistics add) and Oregon/Arizona wastewater limits on copper and tungsten all add friction ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/united-states-cmp-slurries-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)). A four-person team is thin for multi-fab support, Korean trade-secret prosecutions in the slurry industry will surface in U.S. due diligence ([Biz Hankook](https://bizhankook.com)), and Chinese low-price competition in mature-node slurry plus annual price negotiations compress the margin the model assumes ([Growth Stock Insight](https://growthstockinsight.com/cmp-슬러리-성장주-계속-오를까-첨단-공정-물량-822e0db3/)). A Korean-origin location question also remains open: the client's Portland headquarters differs from the registered **KCTech America address at 5501 NE Century Blvd., Hillsboro, OR 97124**, incorporated April 2025 ([kctech.com](https://www.kctech.com/eng/page/location3.php); [The Bell](https://www.thebell.co.kr/front/newsview.asp?code=00&key=202510231531579400106938)) — Hillsboro is in the Portland metro but a separate city, one aggregator still calls the location unresolved ([Korean Tech Texas](https://koreantechtexas.com/kctech-cmp-dual-pattern-samsung-partner-day/)), and this report uses Hillsboro because the company's own site and the trade press agree.

## Conclusion

The branch is a qualification business, not a sales business. A $15M fourth-year run rate requires roughly three qualified fabs at 8–15% share each, which in turn requires one Intel application family to enter production by mid-2027 and a second anchor — most plausibly Samsung Taylor on the back of Korean qualification — by 2028. Every assumption that could pull that forward is worth more than any pricing concession: a toll-blend line in Portland, an early second-source slot on an already-qualified ceria chemistry, and an applications engineer embedded in Intel's Hillsboro CMP module. Every assumption that pushes it back — a missed 2026–27 process-of-record window, a customer audit that penalises import-only supply, or an exclusion order — pushes first meaningful revenue to 2029.

Two planning implications follow. Fund the branch as a US$1.3–1.8M-a-year option that breaks even in 2028–29 at the earliest, and do not commit to a U.S. plant before an anchor customer's letter of intent, as Soulbrain's stalled Taylor project shows. And separate the two questions the client has merged: the U.S. equipment opportunity is live today and already attached to a Georgia glass-substrate line, while the slurry opportunity is a two-to-three-year qualification project whose payoff is real but back-loaded. The strongest strategic argument for slurry in Oregon is not market size but proximity to the fab cluster where Intel's CMP specifications are written.
