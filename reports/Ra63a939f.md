# Targeting Second-Source Slurry Share Yields Modest Revenue

KCTech's U.S. slurry branch is most likely to reach **about $4–5M in annual revenue by 2028–2029** if it wins roughly 1% of the U.S. CMP slurry market, with a plausible range of **$1.5M to $10M** depending on share. The base market is the only internally consistent U.S. figure available: **[Straits Research](https://straitsresearch.com/report/cmp-slurry-market)** puts U.S. CMP slurry at $360M in 2025 and $390M in 2026, and reports a 9.8% North American CAGR. Revenue in 2026 is unlikely because qualification typically takes **6–18 months** from first sample to volume order ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/aluminum-cmp-slurry-market-forecast-to-2035-demand-accelerates-on-semiconductor-fab-expansion/); [Jeez Semicon](https://jeez-semicon.com/blog/top-cmp-slurry-manufacturers-suppliers-in-2026-a-complete-procurement-guide/)). A cross-check from a single fab points to the same order of magnitude: a 30,000-wafer-per-month site could spend roughly $22–88M a year on slurry, so a 10–20% second-source position would be worth about $2–18M. The estimates are sensitive to unresolved conflicts in the market data: global 2025 sizes range from $1.69B to $2.6B, and U.S. import shares are unverified. No internal Korean-branch deal sizes were available, so the revenue figures rest on market-share assumptions rather than KCTech's own pricing history.

## U.S. slurry demand is large but its size is uncertain

Global CMP slurry estimates for 2025 cluster around $2.1B, but the spread is wide. Among seven slurry-only estimates, the values run from **$1.69B** ([Maximize Market Research](https://www.maximizemarketresearch.com/market-report/global-cmp-slurry-market/25251/)) to **$2.60B** ([Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/cmp-slurry-market-115384)), and the median is about $2.1B (my calculation). Forecast growth is also uncertain, with CAGRs from 4.8% to 8.6% depending on the source. SEMI's slurry-plus-pad figure is **$3.5B for 2025** ([SEMI](https://www.semi.org/sites/semi.org/files/2025-09/5%20Clark%20Tseng_Building%20the%20Future-AI%20Investment,%20Equipment%20&%20Materials%20Market%20Outlook.pdf)), which is consistent with pads being a large share of that total, though SEMI does not split it.

U.S. figures are scarce. Dimension Market Research gives a $1.7B U.S. figure that cannot be reconciled with its own $6.5B global number, so the Straits $360M is the only usable base. Using it, the U.S. market is about **$390M in 2026**; extending at 9.8% gives roughly **$428M in 2027, $470M in 2028 and $516M in 2029** (my calculations; Straits does not state a U.S.-specific CAGR). Asia-Pacific holds the largest share in every source, but the figures conflict sharply: [Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/cmp-slurry-market-115384) gives **76.9%**, while [Dataintelo](https://dataintelo.com/report/global-cmp-slurry-market) gives **44.5%**. This matters because the U.S. share is not reported directly.

Demand that the U.S. base reflects is concentrated in a few fabs. **TSMC Arizona's** first fab has been in high-volume N4 production since Q4 2024, and its second fab is slated for volume N3 production in the second half of 2027 ([Black Ridge Research](https://www.blackridgeresearch.com/project-profiles/tsmc-arizona-fab-united-states-us-details-cost-expansion-latest-update); [Manufacturing Mag](https://www.manufacturingmag.com/article/tsmc-arizona-timeline-forward-3nm-2027-165b-six-fab)). **Samsung Taylor** is targeting full operation by end-2026 ([KXAN](https://www.kxan.com/news/business/new-texas-samsung-chip-plant-2nm/)), but customer production is now stated for 2027 ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317402/20260531/samsung-taylor-fab-production-confirmed-2027-sf2p-2nm-process-goes.htm)), and a March 2026 report said full-scale mass production slipped to early 2027 ([Semicon Electronics](https://www.semicone.com/article-401.html)). **Intel Ohio** is delayed to 2030–2031 and contributes little within the forecast window ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/21/intel-hits-construction-milestone-at-delayed-ohio-chip-fab-.html)), while **Intel Arizona's** next fab is expected to operate by the end of 2028 (same source). No source gave verified status for Micron, GlobalFoundries or Texas Instruments U.S. sites.

## A new entrant's share implies single-digit millions

The revenue scenarios below apply share assumptions to the Straits-based U.S. market. The base case assumes a 1% share, which is plausible for a secondary supplier with several qualified fabs; the low and high cases assume 0.3% and 2%.

| Year | U.S. market (calc.) | Low (0.3%) | Base (1%) | High (2%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 | $390M | ~$0 (qualification) | ~$0 | ~$0 |
| 2027 | ~$428M | ~$1.3M | ~$4.3M | ~$8.6M |
| 2028 | ~$470M | ~$1.4M | ~$4.7M | ~$9.4M |
| 2029 | ~$516M | ~$1.5M | ~$5.2M | ~$10.3M |

Source for the market base: [Straits Research](https://straitsresearch.com/report/cmp-slurry-market). The table values are my calculations, not sourced forecasts.

Two further anchors bracket these numbers. The import share matters: an industry summary estimates that **35–45% of U.S. CMP consumption by volume is imported**, mainly from Japan and South Korea ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/united-states-cmp-materials-for-semiconductor-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)). That share is not backed by trade data, but if accurate, applying it to the 2025 U.S. value implies about **$126–162M** of imported slurry that a Korean supplier could theoretically displace. Per-fab economics also help. Slurry use per wafer is reported at about **0.2–1.0 L per CMP step**, and one formula implies about 12.3 L per wafer ([Benner](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/CMPUG524-12-Benner-ConfluenseLLC.pdf)). A vendor blog gives **$5–20 per litre** for silicon-wafer slurry ([Jeez Semicon](https://jeez-semicon.com/blog/cmp-cost-optimization-how-to-reduce-slurry-consumption-and-improve-yield/)), which is far from a verified price. At 30,000 wafers per month, that is roughly $22–88M of slurry a year per fab (my calculation). Winning 10–20% of a fab's volume as a secondary source would then be worth about $2–18M a year at that fab.

Intel is the most natural first customer, but its slurry spend is not disclosed. Intel guided 2026 gross capex to **$20B**, up from about $18B, and said 2027 capex would rise strongly, mostly for equipment ([BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/quote/INTC/earnings-call/US_INTC_2026-10-29); [CoinDCX](https://coindcx.com/blog/us-stock/intel-q3-2026-earnings-preview)). That supports the view that wafer volume will grow, but no source ties this to slurry purchases. Intel also expects no positive free cash flow in 2027, which may make it more cost-focused on suppliers (inference).

## Competitors and Oregon rivals set a high entry bar

Market shares are unreliable. Entegris, which absorbed CMC Materials, is most often cited as the leader at about **23% global share** ([Fact.MR](https://www.factmr.com/report/4480/cmp-slurry-market)). Other estimates put the top five at **65–70%** ([Jeez Semicon](https://jeez-semicon.com/blog/top-cmp-slurry-manufacturers-suppliers-in-2026-a-complete-procurement-guide/)) or about **67%** ([OpenPR](https://www.openpr.com/news/4612477/global-cmp-slurry-market-size-is-expected-to-reach-usd-2-676-46)), and another report says the top three hold about 51%. One aggregator lists a leader structure that double-counts Cabot and Entegris, so it is low reliability. The practical reading is that a new entrant faces a concentrated market where the top players hold most volume.

Entegris reported 2025 Materials Solutions sales of **$1,406.7M**, but that is a segment total covering slurries, pads and other materials, not slurry revenue ([Entegris ARS FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1101302/000110130226000055/ars2025final.pdf)). Qnity, the DuPont electronics business, launched its **Stackplane** slurry platform for advanced packaging on August 31, 2026 ([Qnity](https://ir.qnityelectronics.com/press-releases/detail/76/qnity-introduces-stackplane-cmp-slurry-platform-for-advanced-packaging)). Fujifilm, Fujimi, Resonac and 3M are also listed among key players, though no U.S. slurry share is reported for any of them.

Korean rivals are more relevant to KCTech's positioning. **Soulbrain** is building a phosphoric acid plant in East Taylor, Texas, about four miles from Samsung's Taylor fab; Phase 1 is about $175M with completion in 2029 ([Korean Tech Texas](https://koreantechtexas.com/soulbrain-taylor-grant-subsidy-visibility/); [C&EN](https://cen.acs.org/materials/electronic-materials/Soulbrain-plans-electronic-chemical-plant/102/i26)). That is a chemical plant, not slurry, but it signals that Korean suppliers are following customers into Texas. KCTech itself has been sole slurry supplier for Absolics' glass-substrate line, and its vice chairman said in 2025 that it plans a local U.S. office and sees potential with GlobalFoundries, Intel and CXMT ([The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=13280); [The Worldfolio](https://www.theworldfolio.com/interviews/Next-Gen_CMP_Ventus_System_and_Ceria_Slurry_for_Advanced_2nm_Node_Chips/6973/)). These are stated aims, not confirmed qualifications.

The most important Oregon rival is **AGC Electronics America**, which says its CES-300/330F CMP slurry is made in Hillsboro, Oregon ([AGCEA](https://www.agcem.com/industries/&gsessionid=D5VKyIMQaIoT_FeFACP-XGK2jqK6kLsmrX3DaLip7BBwVEQZsimjnQ)). Kanto and High Purity Products are Portland chemical makers, but their slurry activity is unconfirmed. There is also a legal constraint: the ITC found that DuPont's Optiplane 2300 and 2600 dielectric slurries infringed a CMC patent, and an exclusion order blocks their U.S. import until 2035 ([CMC Materials 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1102934/000110293421000062/ccmp-20211216xex991.htm)). KCTech should check its dielectric formulations against Entegris patents before importing them.

## Qualification, costs and policy shape the first two years

Qualification is the binding constraint. Vendor material gives **3–9 months** for mature-node slurry qualification and **12–24 months** of engineering time at leading-edge fabs, with **$500,000–$2,000,000** in direct qualification cost, though the latter figure has no cited study ([Jeez Semicon](https://jeez-semicon.com/blog/top-cmp-materials-suppliers-2025-comparison/)). Fabs typically keep a primary supplier at 80–90% of volume and a qualified secondary at 10–20% ([Jeez Semicon](https://jeez-semicon.com/blog/colloidal-silica-slurry-suppliers-how-to-evaluate-quality-consistency-and-technical-support/)). Those figures make the second-source role the realistic opening, and they explain why the base-case share is 1%. No primary Intel, TSMC, Samsung or GlobalFoundries document gives a qualification timeline.

Operating costs are only partly sourced. Average Portland office rent is about **$22 per square foot per year** ([CommercialCafe](https://www.commercialcafe.com/office/us/or/portland/)), so a 1,500 sq ft office would cost about $33,000 a year before fit-out (my calculation). Serviced space is available from **$600 per month** per desk ([Kiln](https://kiln.com/communities/portland/)), which suits a four-person team in the first year. Lab access is cheaper than most: Oregon State's MaSC charges external users a **$510** training fee and **$400 per month** per researcher ([OSU MaSC](https://research.engr.oregonstate.edu/masc/become-user)). Salaries, insurance, entity formation and hazmat permits were not sourced, so a full budget cannot be built from the evidence.

Policy adds uncertainty rather than a clear barrier. The Section 232 semiconductor tariff of 25% covers only a narrow class of advanced chips, and no source confirms that slurries fall within it ([Tariffos](https://tariffos.com/en/measures/section-232-semiconductors); [FindChips](https://blog.findchips.com/semiconductor-tariffs-2026/)). A broader second phase has been recommended but not enacted ([GingerControl](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/section-232-semiconductor-tariff)). Korean goods fall under a 15% framework ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/11/united-states-korea-trade-deal-framework.html)), but sources disagree on which reciprocal tariffs remain after the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling, so customs-broker confirmation is needed. CHIPS-linked incentives exclude "foreign entities of concern," and Korea is not on the countries-of-concern list in the sources ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/blogs/perspectives-innovation/guardrails-chips-act-funding-restrict-investments-china-may-restrict)). Oregon offers a **$240 million** Oregon CHIPS Fund ([Business Oregon](https://www.oregon.gov/biz/Publications/releases/Semiconductor%20Firms%20Grow%20in%20Oregon.pdf)) and a 15% semiconductor R&D credit, though that figure comes from a commercial summary and should be checked against statute ([Swanson Reed](https://www.swansonreed.com/research-tax-credit/oregon/glossary/oregon-semiconductor-rd-tax-credit)).

Staffing carries the most immediate uncertainty. The $100,000 H-1B payment was vacated in June 2026 according to USCIS ([USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/alerts/h-1b-faq)), but later extension and invalidation reports give conflicting dates ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/100k-h-1b-fee-extended-through-2027)), and a $103,265 fee was proposed in August 2026 with no final rule. The team should not rely on H-1B hiring until this is settled. E-2 and L-1 options were not researched.

## Customer acquisition: conventional routes and unconventional bets

**Conventional routes** are the best-documented. Intel requires RBA Code of Conduct conformance, may run a cybersecurity risk assessment at onboarding, and expects suppliers to attest to controls ([Intel](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/supplier/supply-chain-security.html); [Intel](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/supplier/corporate-responsibility.html)). Its Graphite Connect platform is invitation-only, so a supplier cannot self-register ([Intel](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/supplier/graphite-connect.html)). Sample shipment and lot-to-lot trials, typically across five or more lots for tungsten-type slurries, are the core of qualification ([Jeez Semicon](https://jeez-semicon.com/blog/top-cmp-slurry-manufacturers-suppliers-in-2026-a-complete-procurement-guide/)). Personal networks from Intel, GlobalFoundries and Coherent alumni are plausible, but no source measures their effectiveness. Trade shows are available: SEMICON West 2026 runs October 13–15 in San Francisco, and moves to Phoenix from 2027 ([SEMI](https://www.semi.org/en/semi-press-release/semicon-west-2026-to-spotlight-1-trillion-dollars-semiconductor-milestone-and-technologies-powering-the-industrys-next-era)).

**Unconventional routes** are less proven but have some support. The strongest is following Korean customers abroad. Soulbrain's strategy is to follow Korean customers' overseas plants and then win other chipmakers locally ([Koala Gains](https://koalagains.com/stocks/KOSDAQ/357780/future-performance)). KCTech's Samsung and SK hynix relationships at home could support a Samsung Taylor approach, although no source confirms a Taylor slurry supply. A second route is the supply gap at TSMC. An analysis of TSMC's overseas fabs found that most of its raw-material suppliers had not yet set up Arizona facilities ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/tsmc-overseas-fabs-a-success)), and TSMC runs programs to develop local chemical suppliers and reports second-sourcing as a common challenge ([TSMC](https://esg.tsmc.com/file/public/e-AResponsiblePurchaser_1.pdf); [Arizona Technology Council](https://www.aztechcouncil.org/tsmc-gives-insight-into-arizona-factory-to-compete-for-federal-chips-funding/)). A Portland supply point could therefore be a credible pitch. A third route is university reference work. Oregon State's cleanroom and MaSC facility are small, and no source confirms CMP tools there, but they can support R&D and produce reference data ([OSU](https://engineering.oregonstate.edu/research/semiconductor)). A fourth is niche product focus: advanced-packaging and hybrid-bonding slurries are emerging, which lets a newcomer compete where incumbents are less entrenched, and KCTech is already developing hybrid-bonding slurry ([The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=10059)). None of these routes has a documented success rate.

## Conclusion

The branch's realistic opportunity is a second-source slurry position in the U.S., built around TSMC Arizona, Samsung Taylor and, later, Intel Arizona. Revenue of low single-digit millions in 2027 and roughly $5M by 2029 is a defensible planning base, with upside if a primary qualification lands at a large fab. The downside is that qualification delays could push meaningful revenue past 2028, and a single fab slip, such as Samsung's, would remove a large share of the near-term opportunity.

Three actions reduce the largest risks. First, the team should confirm the import share and U.S. slurry market size with trade data, because the current base rests on one research firm. Second, it should check whether its dielectric formulations fall under the DuPont exclusion order and confirm the tariff stack with a customs broker before committing to an import-based price model. Third, it should pursue a Samsung Taylor or TSMC Arizona reference alongside Intel, since those fabs are closer to volume production in the forecast window.
