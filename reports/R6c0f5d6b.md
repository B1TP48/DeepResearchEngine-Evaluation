# Qualification Timing Will Set KCTech's U.S. Revenue

Two premises in the brief are contradicted or unconfirmed. KCTech describes its equipment business as a leading line, not an immature one ([The Worldfolio interview, 2025](https://www.theworldfolio.com/interviews/Next-Gen_CMP_Ventus_System_and_Ceria_Slurry_for_Advanced_2nm_Node_Chips/6973/)), though that is the company's own account. No source confirms Dr. Sunghoon Lee's promotion to U.S. branch CEO or a U.S. branch start in late 2025. On the market side, Straits Research values the U.S. CMP slurry market at **$360 million in 2025**, rising to **$390 million in 2026** ([Straits Research](https://straitsresearch.com/report/cmp-slurry-market)). Assuming **0.5–2% share by 2027**, rising to **2–10% by 2030** and applied to the oxide-slurry segment (41.5% of the market), the branch's base case is about **$1.8 million in 2027, $4.9 million in 2028, $8.6 million in 2029 and $11.8 million in 2030**, with a range of about $4.7–23.5 million by 2030. These are scenarios built on assumed shares, not sourced forecasts. No KCTech slurry price, deal size or U.S. customer order was found, and the binding constraint is the time to a first qualified order. Qualification at a leading fab is commonly put at six months or longer, and at up to two years for leading-edge work, so near-term revenue is likely to be small. Intel is plausible but unconfirmed as a customer.

## Key terms and what the market measures

**CMP slurry** is a liquid suspension of abrasive particles in chemicals, used to polish wafer layers flat during chip manufacturing; abrasive particles and impurities can cause scratches and yield loss ([Applied Physics USA](https://appliedphysicsusa.com/why-cmp-slurry-monitoring-and-particle-size-analysis-matter-in-semiconductor-manufacturing/)). Slurries are classed by abrasive, such as ceria or silica for oxide layers and metal slurries for copper and tungsten ([Jeez Semicon](https://jeez-semicon.com/blog/cmp-slurry-types-composition-particle-size-and-selection-guide/)). **Qualification** is the customer's process of testing and approving a new supplier's material on production tools ([Execgraph Energy](https://execgraphenergy.com/blog/samsung-taylor-texas-fab-vendor-opportunities)). **Yearly revenue** in this report means the branch's annual slurry sales to U.S. customers; the notes give no exchange rate, so won figures are not converted to dollars.

## U.S. demand is real but its size is unresolved

Market-research estimates of U.S. CMP slurry demand conflict by a factor of about five. Straits Research gives $360 million for 2025 ([Straits Research](https://straitsresearch.com/report/cmp-slurry-market)), Market Growth Reports gives a U.S. segment of $450 million ([Market Growth Reports](https://www.marketgrowthreports.com/market-reports/cmp-slurry-and-pads-market-116426)), and Dimension Market Research gives **$1.7 billion for 2025**, though its own global figures are internally inconsistent ([Dimension Market Research](https://dimensionmarketresearch.com/report/cmp-slurry-market/)). Global 2025 estimates cluster around $2.1–2.4 billion, with Transparency Market Research at $2.1 billion ([TMR](https://www.transparencymarketresearch.com/cmp-slurry-market.html)), Dataintelo at $2.40 billion ([Dataintelo](https://dataintelo.com/report/global-chemical-mechanical-planarization-cmp-slurry-sales-market)) and SNS Insider at $2.42 billion ([SNS Insider](https://www.snsinsider.com/reports/cmp-slurry-market-11235)). Because these are syndicated estimates, the low end ($360 million) is the planning base and $1.7 billion is the upside test.

Demand is tied to specific fabs. Intel's Arizona Fab 52 has been ramping on 18A since October 2025 ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/intels-fab-roadmap-examined)), and Fab 62 is expected to run by end-2028 ([ABC15](https://www.abc15.com/news/business/intels-sixth-arizona-chip-factory-moves-forward-as-ceo-pushes-foundry-turnaround)). Samsung's Taylor Fab 1 is scheduled for customer production in 2027, and GlobalFoundries' Malta fab for 2029 ([Manufacturing Magazine](https://www.manufacturingmag.com/article/intel-samsung-or-globalfoundries-which-us-fab-ramps-first)). Intel's Ohio One is not expected to start production until 2030 or 2031 ([WindowsForum](https://windowsforum.com/windows-news.4/intel-ohio-one-fab-wont-start-production-until-2030.443578/)). Federal support is real but lagging: GAO reports **$13.1 billion disbursed as of April 2026** against **$31.2 billion in direct awards**, with Intel receiving $7.865 billion ([GAO-26-109121](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-109121/index.html)). No Oregon-specific fab construction date was found. Intel's Oregon plans are mentioned, but no start date or capacity is given ([The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=14870)).

Slurry consumption per fab is poorly documented. An older patent describes **400–600 mL per polish cycle** ([USPTO 5,664,990](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5664990)), and a 2024 CMP Users Group presentation assumes **0.3 L per wafer pass** ([CMPUG 2024](https://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/CMPUG524-12-Benner-ConfluenseLLC.pdf)). Combining the vendor figure of 50,000 wafers a month at 1–3 L per wafer gives about **0.6–1.8 million litres a year** for one mid-sized 300 mm fab. At the vendor's $5–20 per litre ([Jeez Semicon](https://jeez-semicon.com/blog/cmp-cost-optimization-how-to-reduce-slurry-consumption-and-improve-yield/)), that is roughly $3–36 million a year in slurry spend, a derived figure using unverified prices.

## Competitors hold the qualified positions

Market-share data conflict too much to plan against. Entegris (CMC) is estimated at about **23%** of global slurry ([JEEZ Semicon](https://jeez-semicon.com/blog/top-cmp-slurry-manufacturers-global-supplier-guide-2026/)), at **8–11%** in another estimate ([Faxiangongchang](https://faxiangongchang.com/en/reports/china-cmp-pad-slurry-2026)), and at **30% or more** in a third ([SemiconductorX](https://semiconductorx.com/semiconductor-cmp-slurries.html)). The top five suppliers are put at 65–70% in one guide ([JEEZ Semicon](https://jeez-semicon.com/blog/top-cmp-slurry-manufacturers-suppliers-in-2026-a-complete-procurement-guide/)) and 85–90% in another ([SemiconductorX](https://semiconductorx.com/semiconductor-cmp-slurries.html)). Fujifilm has the clearest U.S. slurry footprint. It said in October 2026 that it would spend **$18 million** to expand copper-interconnect CMP slurry capacity at Mesa, Arizona, with new equipment scheduled to run in 2027 ([Fujifilm Newsroom US](https://newsroom-us.fujifilm.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/Fujifilm-to-Expand-CMP-Slurry-Production-Capacity-in-the-United-States/default.aspx); [IBTimes](https://jp.ibtimes.com/fujifilm-plans-18-million-expansion-cmp-slurry-production-104738)). Its 2022 programme was **$350 million**, including an **$88 million** Mesa expansion ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/de/news-release/2022/07/13/2479055/0/en/Fujifilm-Announces-350-Million-Investment-in-its-U-S-Electronic-Materials-Business-by-Early-2024.html)). Entegris announced **$700 million** for a U.S. technology centre in Illinois covering slurries, but this is R&D and technology spend, not stated slurry capacity ([Entegris IR](https://investor.entegris.com/news/news-details/2025/Entegris-Announces-Plans-for-700-Million-Investment-in-the-United-States-Technology-Center-in-Illinois/default.aspx)).

Qualification time is disputed, and the sources sort into two groups. A Samsung Austin supplier guide puts qualification of critical process materials, including CMP slurries, at **6–18 months** ([Execgraph Energy](https://execgraphenergy.com/blog/samsung-taylor-texas-fab-vendor-opportunities)), and an industry article describes swapping in a material from a different supplier as an **18-month** requalification ([IndustryWeek](https://www.industryweek.com/supply-chain/planning-forecasting/article/55383793/what-semiconductor-supply-chains-can-teach-the-rest-of-manufacturing)). Market-research releases instead put leading-edge qualification at **2–4 years** and costing tens of millions of dollars ([PR Newsreleaser](https://www.prnewsreleaser.com/news/138386)), and above 24 months for a new chemical ([24chemicalresearch](https://www.24chemicalresearch.com/reports/223838/united-states-semiconductor-fabrication-chemicals-market)). The report follows the shorter range for adding a supplier to an existing node, because it matches the one primary document in the notes: CMC's 10-K says slurry and pad qualification "may take six months or longer" ([CMC Materials 10-K FY2020](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1102934/000110293420000055/ccmp-20200930.htm)). The longer range applies to a new chemistry at a new node, which is a plausible route for KCTech's copper slurry.

## Revenue estimate for the first five years

The estimate uses the Straits base market, grown at its stated 9.8% North American rate, and applies the oxide share as the addressable segment. The notes do not give a U.S.-specific oxide share, so the 41.5% figure is an assumption borrowed from the global mix ([Straits Research](https://straitsresearch.com/report/cmp-slurry-market)). The timing is driven by the qualification evidence. A supplier that began sampling in late 2025 would, on the 6–18-month range, reach a first qualified order around 2026–2027 at the earliest. That places the first meaningful revenue in 2027–2028, and Samsung Taylor or Intel Fab 62 timing would push volume toward 2028 onward. The 2026 figure is zero because no qualified order was found. Using the $1.7 billion Dimension base instead would roughly quadruple every row, but that figure is an outlier against the other publishers. A 10% share by 2030 would be aggressive for a new entrant against Entegris and Fujifilm.

The Korean benchmarks anchor the estimate on the company side. KCTech's 2021 total revenue was **KRW 344.5 billion** ([KCTech equity research, 2022](https://ssl.pstatic.net/imgstock/upload/research/company/1665097269003.pdf)), and a 2022 broker estimated its 2021 semiconductor slurry sales at **KRW 110–120 billion**, which is an estimate, not a filed figure. Slurry was forecast at KRW 130 billion for 2022 and KRW 150 billion for 2023 ([Insight Korea, Kiwoom note](http://www.insightkorea.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=102310)). Total 2025 revenue was **KRW 382.9 billion** ([MoatMap](https://moatmap.ai/deep-dive/281820.KS)), and an aggregator puts slurry at about 40% of sales, which would imply roughly KRW 150 billion; this share is unverified. Against that, KCTech's U.S. sales were **KRW 2.93 billion in Q1 2026**, or **1.9%** of revenue, with 89.2% from South Korea ([The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=10433)). The U.S. branch therefore starts from a small base. No average slurry order size or deal size was found for any year. The only disclosed deal sizes are equipment contracts, such as **KRW 21.1 billion** from SK hynix ([TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/kctech-receives-krw-21-1-billion-semiconductor-equipment-order-from-sk-hynix)), and these are not slurry prices.

Price per litre is the largest unknown in converting share to revenue. One China report gives **$30–100 per litre** on average, with mature-node oxide at **$20–30** ([Indexbox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/china-cmp-slurries-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)), while a procurement blog gives **$5–20** ([Jeez Semicon](https://jeez-semicon.com/blog/cmp-cost-optimization-how-to-reduce-slurry-consumption-and-improve-yield/)). The North American per-kilogram series was **$36.1/kg in 2023** ([Reports and Data](https://www.reportsanddata.com/report-detail/chemical-mechanical-planarization-cmp-slurry-market)). Because the price basis differs, the share-based revenue figures above are the more defensible output, and price should be checked against KCTech's own quotes.

## Costs, barriers and risks

The fixed-cost base is modest but not trivial. Portland's average office asking rent was **$28.92 per sq ft a year** in Q3 2026, with Class A at **$34.68** and Class B at **$27.12** ([Kidder Mathews](https://kidder.com/market-reports/portland-office-market-report/)). For an assumed 3,000 sq ft office, that is roughly **$81,000–104,000 a year** before fit-out and utilities; the space size is an assumption. Researcher pay is the other fixed cost. A Portland semiconductor engineer's average is **$85,880** ([Salary.com](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/recruiting/semiconductor-engineer-salary/portland-or)), and a process engineer's median is **$152,780** ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/portland-semiconductor-process-engineer-salary-SRCH_IL.0,8_IM700_KO9,39.htm)). Two researchers would therefore cost about **$170,000–310,000 a year**. No source gives salaries for sales or application roles, lab or cleanroom leases, or the CEO's pay, so the true fixed base is higher. Against a base-case 2027 revenue of about $1.8 million, the branch would need a gross margin the notes do not provide to cover this.

Oregon offers a partial offset. The state's **15% semiconductor R&D credit** is refundable and requires registration as a qualified semiconductor company and an annual pre-application, but it covers qualified research expenses rather than sales staff ([Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.com/insights/new-oregon-semiconductor-r-and-d-credit-faq); [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/tax-insights-and-commentary/oregons-new-r-d-tax-credit-a-boon-for-eligible-state-businesses)). Whether a foreign parent's U.S. subsidiary qualifies is not shown in the sources. The Oregon CHIPS Fund, by contrast, is tied to manufacturers applying for federal CHIPS dollars, with **eleven agreements worth $195,345,000** ([Business Oregon](https://www.oregon.gov/biz/programs/or_chips_fund/pages/default.aspx)), so a materials-sales branch is unlikely to qualify.

Tariffs are unresolved for slurry specifically. Semiconductors under HTS 8541 and 8542 face a **25% Section 232 tariff** from January 2026 ([GingerControl](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/importing-from-south-korea-tariff-guide)). Calculators disagree on chemicals: one gives about **13%** effective duty for Korean chemicals ([TariffsTool](https://www.tariffstool.com/tariffs-on-chemicals-from-south-korea)), and another lists a **3.5% MFN** rate with no Section 232 layer ([CalcMyTariff](https://www.calcmytariff.com/country/south-korea)). The slurry's HTS code decides which applies, and no source gives it. The IEEPA reciprocal tariff was struck down in February 2026, and the 2025 IEEPA payments are claimable ([TariffsTool](https://www.tariffstool.com/tariffs-on-chemicals-from-south-korea)).

Chemical compliance is a gate. Under TSCA, importing counts as manufacturing, so a substance not on the TSCA Inventory needs a pre-manufacture notice filed **at least 90 days** before the first import ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/reviewing-new-chemicals-under-toxic-substances-control-act-tsca/filing-pre-manufacture-notice-epa)). EPA finalised significant new use rules for 19 chemicals, effective **December 14, 2026**, with semiconductor manufacturing among the designated uses ([IHMM](https://ihmm.org/epa-final-tsca-significant-new-use-rules-for-19-chemicals/)). Whether any KCTech substance is affected was not found. Supply risk centres on ceria: suppliers describe **China** as the dominant source of cerium oxide, which is a high-risk input, while silica is low-risk ([JEEZ Semicon](https://jeez-semicon.com/blog/what-are-cmp-materials-complete-guide/); [JEEZ Semicon, abrasives](https://jeez-semicon.com/blog/cmp-slurry-abrasives-explained-silica-vs-alumina-vs-ceria/)). Those ratings are qualitative, and no quantified shortage data since 2022 was found.

## Customer acquisition and regional competition

Conventional routes are the expected path. Sample trials and formal qualification are the only route to a qualified order, and the qualification evidence above shows they take many months. Personal and Korean-parent connections are the most direct door. Samsung and SK hynix supply more than 90% of KCTech's revenue ([Pinpoint News](https://www.pinpointnews.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=339606)), and Samsung's Taylor fab, with customer production scheduled for 2027, is the nearest fab where those relationships apply. Trade shows are the other conventional channel. SEMICON West runs **October 13–15, 2026** in San Francisco ([SEMI Expo](https://expo.semi.org/west2026/public/MainHall.aspx?ID=32429)), and Semicon Taiwan drew **more than 100,000 attendees in 2025** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semicon_Taiwan)). The notes give no exhibiting cost or conversion rate for either.

Less usual routes may matter more. First, Intel has publicly prioritised regional sourcing and emerging-supplier growth. It gave Fujifilm an EPIC award for regional sourcing in 2025 ([Intel Newsroom](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/news/corporate/intel-announces-2025-epic-supplier-award-recipients.html)), and its 2025–26 report aims to double emerging-supplier spend by 2030 ([Intel CSR 2025–26](https://www.intel.la/content/dam/www/central-libraries/us/en/documents/2026-08/csr-2025-26-full-report.pdf)). A Portland team offering local technical support and full chemical disclosure fits that stated direction, though no source says a new supplier qualifies as "emerging" for slurry. Second, a silica-based or non-China ceria offer answers the supply-risk concern above, which is a useful pitch to fabs that worry about rare-earth exposure. Third, a Portland research team could work with a non-Intel process group on a co-development project. The sources describe co-development with foundry process teams as how incumbents hold their positions ([SemiconductorX](https://semiconductorx.com/semiconductor-cmp-slurries.html)). Fourth, existing wet-chemical suppliers already in Texas, such as Soulbrain at Taylor and Dongjin at Killeen, show Korean chemical makers can run U.S. fab relationships, though neither makes slurry there ([Korean Tech Texas](https://koreantechtexas.com/soulbrain-175m-taylor-phosphoric-acid-samsung-supply-chain/); [Dongjin](https://www.dongjin.com/en/company/be_overseas.php)).

Regional competition in Oregon is thin on evidence. No Portland-based slurry competitor was found. KCTech's own Oregon presence is disputed: a 2025 feature says KCTech America Inc. was set up in April 2025 in Hillsboro, Oregon ([The Bell](https://m.thebell.co.kr/m/newsview.asp?svccode=&newskey=202510231531579400106938)), while a MoatMap page describes an Oregon office as planned and not confirmed ([MoatMap](https://moatmap.ai/deep-dive/281820.KS)). The Bell gives a specific date and is more likely right, but no registry check resolves it. One report says Entegris announced a **$200 million** slurry expansion in Oregon in 2023 ([ReportsAndData](https://www.reportsanddata.com/report-detail/chemical-mechanical-planarization-cmp-slurry-market)). That is a single secondary claim, and no primary Entegris disclosure confirms it. If true, Entegris would be a local incumbent in the same region.

## Conclusion

The U.S. slurry opportunity is real but slow to convert. Straits Research's **$390 million 2026 U.S. market** is a conservative base for planning, and the scenario table shows that **$1.8–11.8 million a year** by 2027–2030 is plausible at 1–5% share. Upside cases above that depend on Intel or Samsung qualification and on the higher market figures. The first meaningful revenue is more likely in 2027–2028 than in 2026, and the branch should plan its cash around that gap.

The most useful next steps are evidence gaps the team can close. KCTech should confirm its U.S. legal entity and location, its slurry HTS codes and TSCA inventory status, and its actual price per litre and average order size. It should also test two qualification assumptions with a fab process team: how many months a sample-to-qualified-order cycle takes for a ceria or silica oxide slurry at an existing node, and whether Intel would classify KCTech as a new regional supplier. Until those are answered, the revenue figures above should be read as scenarios, not forecasts.
