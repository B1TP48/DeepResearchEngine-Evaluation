# KCTech's Portland Slurry Branch Can Realistically Reach $3–6M by 2028

KCTech's US materials branch should plan on **$0.1–0.5M of revenue in 2026, $1–2.5M in 2027 and $3–6M in 2028**, with a base case of roughly $0.2M / $1.5M / $4.0M. Those numbers are not a demand constraint — the US CMP slurry market is roughly **$300–360M in 2025** and grows ~8% a year ([SNS Insider](https://www.snsinsider.com/reports/cmp-slurry-market-11235); [TECHCET via Semiconductor Digest](https://www.semiconductor-digest.com/techcet-projects-cmp-market-is-set-to-reach-3-6b-in-2025/)) — but a qualification-clock constraint: a new slurry supplier at a leading-edge fab needs **12–24 months to first production PO and 2–4 years for full adoption** ([JEEZ](https://jeez-semicon.com/de/blog/Top-CMP-Materials-Suppliers-2025-Comparison/)). Against an operating cost base of **~$1.09M a year** plus **~$1.15M of one-time setup**, break-even sits at **~$2.8M of revenue** if the branch books full product margin, so the base case crosses into profit only in 2028 and consumes roughly **$4.1M of cumulative funding** before it does. The two structural levers that decide the outcome are the transfer-pricing structure (a limited-risk reseller earning 8–15% would need $7–12M to break even) and whether local blending is added in Oregon. Intel is the right anchor but the wrong first customer: the fastest technical path runs through Samsung's Taylor, Texas fab, where KCTech is already qualified inside Samsung Korea, and through mature-node fabs in the same metro as the branch.

## KCTech's Korean slurry base is only a $50–100M pool, and two customers own 87% of the company

KCTech reported **KRW 382.9bn revenue and KRW 60.0bn operating profit in FY2025** (15.7% margin), with semiconductors at KRW 345.7bn (90.3%) and display at KRW 37.0bn, down from KRW 76.5bn a year earlier ([DART business report FY2025](https://kind.krx.co.kr/external/2026/03/13/001794/20260313004463/11011.htm)). Slurry is not a disclosed segment, and public estimates of its size conflict materially: a sell-side forecast puts **2025 slurry revenue at KRW 66.1bn, +21% YoY** ([Newstomato](https://www4.newstomato.com)), while a 2021 company statement of "about one third of total revenue" and a 2022 research note citing **KRW 110–120bn of slurry revenue in 2021** ([Paxnet](http://www.paxnet.co.kr/WWW/data/researchCenter/attach/20221007103450615.pdf)) imply a much larger base, and a broker mix estimate of 40% slurry within the semiconductor segment gives **KRW 138bn for FY2025**. The honest range is **KRW 66–138bn (roughly US$47–99M at an assumed 1,400 KRW/USD)**, and the more recent, more specific sell-side number should get more weight — but either way the entire Korean slurry business is a **US$50–100M revenue pool**, which is the correct scale reference for a US branch.

Two facts shape what a US newcomer can sell. First, **Samsung Electronics and SK hynix generated 87% of FY2025 revenue** (KRW 333.7bn of KRW 382.9bn) ([Vom Report](https://vomreport.com)), with 25 and 15 joint development projects respectively ([The Bell](https://www.thebell.co.kr/front/newsview.asp?code=00&key=202510231531579400106938)). Second, the only disclosed single-order values are *equipment* — KRW 69.8bn from SK hynix and a ~KRW 40bn order in August 2026 ([KOSAI](https://kosai.kr/en/stock/281820.html)) — and because Korean rules trigger single-supply-contract disclosure at roughly 5% of revenue (>KRW ~19bn, ~US$14M), **individual slurry contracts are by construction below ~$14M each**. That is a useful ceiling for a first US purchase order.

The product mix also matters. KCTech's strength is **ceria dielectric (STI/oxide) slurry**, dielectric additives, silica dielectric, tungsten and poly-Si slurries, with **copper and next-generation metal slurries still under development** ([Wantstats](https://www.wantstats.com/company-profile/kc-tech)). US leading-edge logic is far more copper/barrier-intensive than Korean memory, so the US-entry portfolio is *narrower* than the Korean revenue base suggests — ceria and tungsten are the sellable beachhead, not a broad CMP portfolio. Existing US revenue is tiny: **KRW 15.5bn in FY2025, 4.05% of total**, up from KRW 14.2bn, against China at 15.4% ([DART business report FY2025](https://kind.krx.co.kr/external/2026/03/13/001794/20260313004463/11011.htm)). KCTech America Inc. was incorporated in **Hillsboro, Oregon in April 2025** for "R&D of slurry and chemical products to strengthen technical responsiveness to US customers" ([DART](https://kind.krx.co.kr/external/2026/03/13/001794/20260313004463/11011.htm); [The Bell](https://www.thebell.co.kr/front/newsview.asp?code=00&key=202510231531579400106938)), sited near Intel "because it is close to Intel's sites" ([The Guru](https://theguru.co.kr/news/article.html?no=90230)).

## The US market is $300–360M, and greater Portland alone accounts for $45–50M

Global CMP slurry was **$2.14B in 2025** (TECHCET) against SNS Insider's **$2.42B**, with an 8.6% and 7.15% CAGR respectively, putting 2030 at **$3.0–3.6B** ([TECHCET](https://techcet.com/2025/11/05/cmp-slurry-and-pads-market-set-for-6-growth-in-2025/); [SNS Insider](https://www.snsinsider.com/reports/cmp-slurry-market-11235)). The US is **$0.360B in 2025 growing 8.30%** (SNS), but trade estimates imply only **8.5–9%** of global, or $180–215M; a **$300M midpoint** with a $250–360M band is the defensible planning figure. Five buyers — Intel, Micron, Samsung Austin, TSMC Arizona and GlobalFoundries — are estimated at **75–80% of US consumption** ([IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/united-states-cmp-slurries-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)).

Consumption per wafer drives everything else. A single CMP step uses **100–300 mL**, a leading-edge die needs **25–41 CMP steps** versus 12–15 at 28nm, DRAM up to 13 and 3XXL 3D NAND up to 36 ([TECHCET via Semiconductor Digest](https://www.semiconductor-digest.com/future-market-trends-for-cmp-consumables-point-upwards/)). Modelled out, that is **$70–90 per leading-edge wafer, ~$30 per DRAM wafer and $6–10 per 200mm analog wafer** — figures derived from step counts and per-step slurry cost of ~$10/litre ([ICPT 2026](https://www.icpt2026.org)) rather than published benchmarks. On that basis the Pacific Northwest punches above its wafer share:

| Region | 2026 ($M) | 2028 ($M) | Share of US |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Pacific Northwest (Oregon + Idaho) | 45–50 | 60–65 | ~14–15% |
| Southwest (AZ, TX, NM) | 185–195 | 260–270 | ~59% |
| Northeast/Midwest (NY, OH, VT) | 60–70 | 85–95 | ~20% |
| West (CA, UT, CO) | 18–22 | 28–33 | ~5–6% |
| **Total (planning midpoint)** | **~355** | **~450** | 100% |

Oregon alone is **~$27–32M in 2026**, of which Intel's Hillsboro D1X campus is 80–85%, with ADI Beaverton, Microchip Gresham, onsemi Gresham and Qorvo making up the rest. The top-down and bottom-up paths diverge by ~10–20% in 2026–2027 (**$323M bottom-up vs $390M top-down** for 2026), so the model should carry a range. Timing matters as much as size: **Intel Ohio is now a 2030–31 event** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/intel-delays-100-billion-ohio-190839996.html)), **GF Malta's first new fab starts production in 2029** ([GlobalFoundries](https://gf.com/zh/manufacturing/malta/malta-expansion/)) and **Micron New York is early 2029 at the earliest** ([DatacenterDynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/micron-to-break-ground-on-new-york-megafab-campus-next-week/)); none of the three belongs in a 2026–2028 revenue model.

## A $0.2M / $1.5M / $4.0M base case, with break-even at ~$2.8M of revenue

Sizing a win is the crux. A single CMP *tool* consumes **$80k–$300k of slurry a year** ([Caladan Semi](https://caladansemi.com)); a full step across 5–15 tools is **$0.5–3M/yr at run-rate**, and a realistic first design win ramps from **$100–500k in the award year to $0.8–1.5M at maturity**. Reaching **$1M needs 1–2 mature wins, $5M needs 3–8, and $10M needs 6–15** — a portfolio, not a product. Because KCTech's addressable niche is ceria STI/ILD (a **$429M segment in 2025 growing 8.64% to $706M by 2031** per [HNY Research via MarketPublishers](https://pdf.marketpublishers.com)) plus tungsten, 1% of that segment is only **$4–7M** — which is exactly the scale a three-person branch can serve.

| Scenario | 2026 | 2027 | 2028 | Implied US share (2028) | Wins needed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Downside | $0.1M | $0.8M | $1.5M | ~0.3% | 1–2 |
| **Base** | **$0.2M** | **$1.5M** | **$4.0M** | **~0.9%** | **3–5** |
| Upside | $0.5M | $2.5M | $6.0–8.0M | ~1.5–1.8% | 6–8 |

The ups**ide requires Intel plus one other account; the base case does not. A reasonable target of **1–3% of US spend by 2028** is **$4–14M**, and anything above 5% ($22M) requires displacing an incumbent at a major leading-edge or memory account.

The cost side is unambiguous. A four-person Hillsboro/Beaverton branch costs **~$1.09M a year** (base case: $747.5k fully loaded payroll at $210k CEO / $135k sales-engineer / 2 × $115k researcher, $72k for 3,000 sq ft of flex lab space, $270k of other overhead), with a low of $898k and a high of $1.40M, plus **~$1.15M of one-time setup** dominated by a $480k lab fit-out and $530k of analytical equipment ([CitySalaryData](https://www.citysalarydata.com); [Norris & Stevens](https://www.norris-stevens.com); [Prisa](https://prisa.jp) for margin norms). At a **39% contribution margin** (42% gross less ~3% freight), break-even is **$2.79M on four FTE**, rising to **$3.4–4.0M** at five to six people.

| Year | Operating cost | Revenue (base) | Contribution | Cash position |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2026 | $1.09M + $1.15M setup | $0.2M | $0.08M | **−$2.30M** |
| 2027 | $1.31M (5 FTE) | $1.5M | $0.59M | **−$1.07M** |
| 2028 | $1.54M (6 FTE) | $4.0M | $1.56M | **−$0.71M** |
| Cumulative | | | | **≈ −$4.1M** |

Two inputs swing this more than any other. **Transfer pricing** decides whether the branch books 40–45% gross margin or an 8–15% reseller markup — the latter triples break-even to **$12.1M**. And **duty**: KORUS eliminates the 5% MFN rate on HTS 3824.99 but does **not** exempt the **10% base reciprocal tariff**, leaving ~10% effective duty on Korean-origin goods ([Tariffstool](https://www.tariffstool.com/guides/tariffs-in-south-korea); [HSCIQ](https://hsciq.com)); a competing secondary source puts Korea in a 12.5% tier, and neither is a USTR or CBP document. At a transfer price of 55–60% of selling price, 10% duty equals **5.5–6.0% of revenue**, roughly 12–14 points of gross margin. Against Chinese slurry facing **25% Section 301 plus reciprocal tariffs**, a KORUS-qualified Korean product carries a structural **~15-point landed-cost advantage** — the single strongest commercial argument for this branch. Working capital is heavy: speciality-chemical terms of **Net 60–90** ([ResolvePay](https://resolvepay.com)) against 100 days of inventory for a 4–6 week ocean lane puts net working capital at **~29% of revenue** ($879k at $3M, $1.47M at $5M).

## Every major incumbent has already localised, and a Texas patent suit shadows the ceria line

The US supply base is denser than KCTech's Oregon footprint. **Fujimi runs plants in Tualatin and Wilsonville — the same metro as the branch** ([Fujimi Corporation](https://www.fujimi.com/about/)); **Fujifilm is investing $18M to expand CMP slurry production in Mesa, Arizona, operating 2027**, co-located with its global CMP slurry R&D centre ([Fujifilm](https://www.fujifilm.com/ro/en/news/hq/14044)); **Entegris opened a $75M-CHIPS-backed Colorado Springs site in November 2025** and committed a further ~$700M to US R&D covering slurries ([TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com)); **Merck/EMD runs production and R&D in Tempe** ([Merck KGaA](https://www.merckgroup.com/press-releases/2021/feb/en/Tempe-Facility-Purchase-EN.pdf)). Korean rivals are following Samsung to Texas: **Soulbrain at ~$549M in Taylor** ([Pulse/MK](https://pulse.mk.co.kr/)) and **Dongjin at $110M+ in Killeen and Plainview, starting Q2–Q3 2026** ([The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=14527)). Local manufacturing is table stakes by 2027, not a differentiator.

Market structure leaves a narrow door. **Fujifilm holds ~31% of global slurry, Entegris second**, with Fujifilm and Entegris still leading in 2025 ([TECHCET](https://www.semiconductorpackagingnews.com/uploads/1/Final_CMP_PR_050124SB.pdf); [Semiconductor Digest](https://semiconductor-digest.com/ai-driven-device-scaling-fuels-next-phase-of-cmp-consumables-growth)); Anji rose from **8% in 2023 to 13% in 2025** ([SemiconductorX](https://semiconductorx.com/semiconductor-cmp-slurries.html)); and in ceria specifically **Resonac, Merck, AGC and KCTech held 84% of a $284M market in 2024** ([Semiconductor Insight](https://semiconductorinsight.com/report/ceria-slurries-market/)). That ceria top-four position is KCTech's genuine asset — and also its legal exposure. **Hitachi Chemical filed a US patent suit against KCTech in a Texas district court** ([AMENews](https://amenews.kr)), and the incumbents enforce hard: CMC/Entegris's ITC exclusion order still bars DuPont's Optiplane 2300/2600 dielectric slurries from the US after a 2024 settlement ([Entegris](https://www.entegris.com/content/dam/web/about-us/news/documents/entegris-settles-patent-dispute-with-dupont-de-nemours-news-release.pdf)), and CMC holds **~255 patents** in the core polishing-composition class versus Anji's 169 ([OnScope](https://onscope.com)). A freedom-to-operate opinion on the specific STI/ILD and tungsten formulations destined for Intel is a precondition, not a formality.

Intel's own process rewards patience and multi-sourcing. Purchasing is **corporate commodity-team-led** with technical sponsorship from the CMP module, suppliers are scored through PQS (80% scorecard) and SCQI (95%+) ([Cabot Microelectronics](https://cabotcmp.com/); [Intel](https://www.intel.com/content/dam/www/central-libraries/us/en/documents/information-for-potential-suppliers.pdf)), materials from a *different site* require "proven equivalence", and **Copy Exactly!** means a slurry qualified in Oregon must reproduce at every Intel site ([Intel materials quality](https://www.datasheetarchive.com/datasheet/a4148002adf5bffa?term=jesd46b)). The three-stage protocol runs **16–28 weeks at a leading-edge logic fab** (20–50 blanket wafers, then 50–150 patterned, then 200–500 product wafers with yield, TDDB and electromigration), compressed to 8–16 weeks at DRAM/mature nodes ([JEEZ](https://jeez-semicon.com/ru/blog/How-to-Select-a-Copper-CMP-Slurry-Specifications-Checklist-Supplier-Evaluation-Guide/)). Intel's historical supplier set — Cabot/CMC, Fujimi, DA NanoMaterials and Moses Lake — shows it does add sources, which is the structural opening. Dr. Sunghoon Lee's profile fits this motion precisely: **15+ years of CMP R&D at Intel and GlobalFoundries, CEO of SMART Pad since 2020, PhD from UC Berkeley** ([SEMICON Korea](https://semiconkorea.org/en/node/6371)), and he presented at the NCCAVS CMP User Group as a **GlobalFoundries BEOL CMP technologist** ([NCCAVS](http://nccavs-usergroups.avs.org/wp-content/uploads/CMPUG2018/CMPUG418-Final-Agenda.pdf)). His "USA branch CEO" title, start date and Coherent/Draper tenures are not confirmed by any public source.

## Sell a narrow ceria beachhead with local blending behind it — not a broad CMP pitch

Conventional tactics are necessary and slow. Sample-and-qualify campaigns cost **$3–8k per account per stage** and consume 270–700 wafers across three stages; ex-fab CMP hires run **$110–150k loaded** plus a 20–25% recruiting fee ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.es)); SEMICON West lists a bare 9 m² booth at **$8,500** ([SEMI](https://expo.semi.org)) but a **KOTRA Korea Pavilion slot subsidises up to 70% of booth rental and fit-out and 100% of 1 CBM freight** for roughly KRW 6M ([Daegu One-Stop](https://onestop119.daegu.go.kr)) — the highest-leverage conventional spend available. Only paid samples generate revenue inside twelve months, and only qualification-lot revenue at that.

| Tactic | Cost | Time to first revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Sample/qualification campaigns | $3–8k per account per stage | 12–24 months |
| CEO network + NCCAVS/technical community | $10–20k/yr travel | 1–3 months to meetings |
| Ex-Intel/GF CMP hire | $110–150k loaded + fee | 3–6 months |
| SEMICON West via KOTRA pavilion | ~$10–25k all-in | 3–9 months to leads |
| Distributor/rep | 3–8% commission | 6–12 months, margin diluted |
| **Oregon blending/finishing suite** | **$1.5–3M capex + $0.8–1.5M/yr** | **18–30 months** |
| **Slurry-as-a-service / per-wafer pricing** | Working capital only | 12–24 months |
| **Licence/blend deal with a Hillsboro formulator** | Low capex | 9–18 months |

The asymmetric plays all remove a reason to say no. **Local blending** is already an operating model in Hillsboro — AGCEA makes ceria CMP slurries (CES-300/CES-330F) at its Oregon plant and ships to global customers ([AGCEA](https://www.agcem.com)) — and it answers both the 10% duty and Intel's "proven equivalence" problem; the sequencing should be to qualify Korean-made material first and localise as a qualified second site, because localisation re-opens qualification. **Per-wafer or slurry-as-a-service pricing** shifts yield risk onto the supplier and is best offered at mature-node accounts (ADI Beaverton, Microchip Gresham, TI Sherman) where yield risk is smaller and the branch learns fab operations cheaply. **Co-development through the CMP technical community** — NCCAVS, CAMP — costs registration and travel and buys credibility a three-person team cannot otherwise get, and the value-selling maths is real: a 1/cm² defect-density reduction is argued to be worth **$0.5–2M per month at 10,000 wafer starts** ([JEEZ](https://jeez-semicon.com/ru/blog/How-to-Select-a-Copper-CMP-Slurry-Specifications-Checklist-Supplier-Evaluation-Guide/)), dwarfing slurry price. What will *not* work is a broad portfolio pitch, because KCTech's copper/barrier products are still in development while US logic demand is copper-heavy.

The risks are concentrated and mostly temporal. **Qualification lock-in** of 12–36 months against a three-person team's burn is the binding constraint, and it is compounded by a corporate rather than local Intel decision, an incumbent capacity race (Fujifilm, Qnity's $1.5bn four-plant programme, KPPC's $120M Casa Grande plant, all aimed at TSMC, Intel and Micron), and Intel's own slippage record. Quality failure is terminal and precedented — a CMP slurry supplier's ISO 9001 certificate was suspended in 2019 for record falsification, forcing customer notification ([Mouser](https://www.mouser.com)). And a failed Intel qualification is not confidential in a community where the same engineers move between Intel, GF, Micron and TI. Sensible commitments: keep Intel below **50% of forecast revenue before 2029**; run one mature-node qualification in parallel from day one specifically for early revenue and learning; pre-negotiate a blending partner before Intel asks; treat the commodity team and Supplier Quality as separate stakeholders; and measure **qualification-stage progression per account per quarter**, not samples shipped — a realistic ladder is 8–12 accounts engaged and 4–6 sample campaigns in year 1, 2–3 evaluations reaching product wafers in year 2, and one completed production qualification plus first repeat orders in year 3.

## Conclusion

The revenue question has a defensible answer and a clear shape: **$0.1–0.5M in 2026, $1–2.5M in 2027, $3–6M in 2028**, with the base case crossing the ~$2.8M break-even line in 2028 and requiring ~$4.1M of cumulative funding. Everything above the base case depends on Intel; everything in the base case depends on Samsung Taylor and a mature-node beachhead. The plan should therefore be underwritten as a three-year, single-anchor, qualification-led investment rather than a sales ramp.

Three implications follow. First, the branch's economics are set by corporate decisions made in Korea, not Oregon: transfer pricing (40%+ margin vs an 8–15% reseller markup) and whether local blending is funded determine viability far more than cost control does. Second, KCTech's genuine differentiation is narrow and legal-risk-bearing — ceria STI/ILD and tungsten, where it sits in a four-supplier 84% concentration — so the pitch should be a second source for two chemistries, not a challenger to Fujifilm and Entegris. Third, the Korean-origin tariff advantage versus Chinese competitors is real, time-limited, and best converted into a domestic blending presence before the window closes in 2027–2028.
