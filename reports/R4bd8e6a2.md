# Build KCTech’s U.S. Slurry Business

The modeled opportunity is a gradual ramp, not an immediate large account: if U.S. selling begins in 2026, a reasonable base case reaches about **$4.5 million in annual revenue by 2029**, with a wide **$1.5–$10 million** range depending on customer qualifications and production ramps. The stated Portland headquarters is not supported by company records: KCTech America is listed at a **Hillsboro, Oregon** address, although Hillsboro is in the Portland metro area ([KCTech 2025 annual report](https://kind.krx.co.kr/external/2026/03/13/001794/20260313004463/11011.htm)). KCTech formed the U.S. subsidiary in 2025 for slurry and chemical R&D and customer support, but public records do not establish a late-2025 commercial launch, the proposed four-person staffing plan, or a confirmed CEO appointment for Dr. Sunghoon Lee. The market is substantial, but published U.S. estimates conflict sharply and KCTech’s Korean filings disclose neither slurry-only sales nor average deal size. The estimates below therefore use explicit customer-slot assumptions rather than treating parent-company revenue as a slurry-sales benchmark. The near-term case rests on winning a small number of product- and process-specific evaluations, with Intel a logical target but not a publicly confirmed U.S. slurry customer.

## KCTech has U.S. R&D and shipments, not proven commercial scale

Here, **CMP slurry** means a liquid abrasive-and-chemical mixture used to planarize semiconductor materials; KCTech’s public portfolio includes ceria and silica slurries, with silica used in applications including metal contacts, plugs, and polysilicon ([KCTech CMP slurry products](https://www.kctech.com/eng/page/product3.php); [KCTech 2025 annual report](https://financialfilings.com/filings/kctech-co-ltd/annual-report/2026/32941200/)). A **qualified product/fab slot** in the revenue model means one slurry formulation approved for one customer fab and process application, not a whole-fab supply award; this is a forecasting unit because public sources do not report standard fab-level deal sizes.

KCTech is a Korean company with established semiconductor equipment and materials businesses. Its **2025 consolidated sales were about KRW 382.9 billion**, of which the semiconductor segment represented **KRW 345.7 billion, or 90.3%**; that segment combines equipment and materials, so it cannot establish slurry sales or the relative maturity of the two businesses ([KCTech 2025 annual report summary](https://financialfilings.com/filings/kctech-co-ltd/annual-report/2026/32941200/)). KCTech America was established on **April 17, 2025**, registered in Oregon on **May 2, 2025**, and listed as a wholly owned R&D subsidiary at **5501 NE Century Blvd., Hillsboro, Oregon** ([KCTech third-quarter filing, November 13, 2025](https://kind.krx.co.kr/external/2025/11/13/000360/20251113000929/11013.htm); [Oregon Secretary of State new-business list, May 2025](https://data.oregon.gov/api/views/qnw2-qahx/rows.pdf?accessType=DOWNLOAD)). Its filings describe slurry and chemical R&D to improve technical support for U.S. customers—not an exclusively sales-focused branch ([KCTech 2025 annual report](https://kind.krx.co.kr/external/2026/03/13/001794/20260313004463/11011.htm)).

The public record indicates progression beyond formation, but not yet a confirmed U.S. production-sales run rate. The 2025 filing reported no U.S. subsidiary sales, and the first-quarter 2026 filing reported no revenue for that quarter; a later 2026 subsidiary summary reports **KRW 488.767 million in revenue**, without identifying the product, customer, or whether it was recurring slurry sales ([KCTech 2025 annual report](https://kind.krx.co.kr/external/2026/03/13/001794/20260313004463/11011.htm); [KCTech first-quarter filing, May 13, 2026](https://kind.krx.co.kr/external/2026/05/13/000950/20260513002079/11013.htm); [KCTech 2026 interim/quarterly report](https://financialfilings.com/filings/kctech-co-ltd/interim-quarterly-report/2026/76225226/)). Customs records show a **200 kg ceria-slurry shipment arriving in June 2026** and a **740 kg additive shipment arriving in September 2026** at KCTech America; they do not establish that either shipment went to a fab or supported a commercial sale ([ImportGenius KCTech America records](https://www.importgenius.com/importers/kctech-america); [ImportKey KCTech America records](https://importkey.com/i/kctech-america-inc)).

The proposed leadership and staffing should be treated as planning assumptions. Public sources associate Sunghoon Lee with KCTech, CMP research, and SMART Pad; a 2021 report supports prior GlobalFoundries experience, but the reviewed evidence does not establish his KCTech America CEO appointment or verify the full claimed Intel, Coherent, and Draper history ([Clarkson University 2024 CMP symposium](https://sites.clarkson.edu/camp/2024-camp-international-cmp-symposium/); [Albany Business Review, February 25, 2021](https://www.bizjournals.com/albany/news/2021/02/25/smart-pad-semiconductor-product-commercialization.html); [UC Berkeley IPIRA—SMART Pad](https://ipira.berkeley.edu/smart-pad-llc)). No public source confirms that the proposed CEO, one sales/research member, and two researchers are in place.

## The market supports a cautious, qualification-led revenue ramp

Market estimates measure different things. Yano reports **$2.183 billion in global CMP-slurry manufacturer shipments for 2025**, while SEMI estimates **$3.5 billion in 2025 for CMP slurry and pads combined**, so SEMI’s broader figure should not be used as slurry-only demand ([Yano Research, Global CMP Slurry Market](https://www.yanoresearch.com/en/press-release/show/press_id/3921); [SEMI Materials Market Outlook](https://www.semi.org/sites/semi.org/files/2025-09/5%20Clark%20Tseng_Building%20the%20Future-AI%20Investment%2C%20Equipment%20%26%20Materials%20Market%20Outlook.pdf)). For U.S. CMP slurry in **2026**, Fortune Business Insights estimates **$370 million**, whereas IndexBox estimates **$1.8–$2.2 billion**; the report uses the Fortune estimate as the more cautious planning reference because the IndexBox figure is close to or greater than several published estimates for the entire global slurry market and is not corroborated by primary supplier or industry sources ([Fortune Business Insights—CMP Slurry Market](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/cmp-slurry-market-115384); [IndexBox—U.S. CMP Slurries Market](https://www.indexbox.io/store/united-states-cmp-slurries-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)). Neither estimate is an audited U.S. total, so this choice is prudent rather than definitive.

Regional estimates also vary: applying reported North American shares of **13.1%–20.1%** to Yano’s **$2.183 billion 2025 global shipment estimate** implies roughly **$286–$439 million** for North America, not the United States alone ([PW Consulting, CMP Slurry for Semiconductor Market](https://pmarketresearch.com/worldwide-cmp-slurry-for-semiconductor-market-research/); [PW Consulting, Global CMP Slurries Market](https://pmarketresearch.com/it/cmp-slurries-market/); [Yano Research](https://www.yanoresearch.com/en/press-release/show/press_id/3921)). Yano’s page also prints an internally inconsistent **$201.2 billion** figure for 2024, despite reporting oxide- and metal-related component values that sum to about **$2.012 billion**; the printed total should not be used ([Yano Research](https://www.yanoresearch.com/en/press-release/show/press_id/3921)). Market size is therefore best used as a scale check, not as a precise revenue forecast.

KCTech does not disclose Korean slurry-only revenue, Korean average deal size, or slurry market share. Its semiconductor-segment sales cannot supply those benchmarks, and the U.S. subsidiary’s reported revenue is not attributable to slurry ([KCTech 2025 annual report](https://kind.krx.co.kr/external/2026/03/13/001794/20260313004463/11011.htm); [KCTech 2026 interim/quarterly report](https://financialfilings.com/filings/kctech-co-ltd/interim-quarterly-report/2026/76225226/)). The following model therefore assumes **$0.25 million, $0.5 million, or $0.8 million per fully ramped qualified product/fab slot per year**—explicit planning inputs, not observed industry averages. It assumes 2025 was setup and R&D, and that commercial selling begins in 2026; a delayed selling start shifts the revenue curve accordingly.

| Calendar year | Low case | Base case | High case |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **2025** | **$0** | **$0** | **$0** |
| **2026** | **$0.05M** | **$0.20M** | **$0.50M** |
| **2027** | **$0.25M** | **$1.00M** | **$2.50M** |
| **2028** | **$0.75M** | **$2.50M** | **$6.00M** |
| **2029** | **$1.50M** | **$4.50M** | **$10.00M** |

These are scenario estimates, not company guidance or reported sales; they assume limited pilot revenue in the first selling year and progressively more active or ramping customer/process slots thereafter. The **$4.5 million 2029 base case** is roughly **1.0%–1.6%** of the estimated **$286–$439 million North American 2025 market**, before market growth; the comparison is a plausibility check, not a measured KCTech share ([Yano Research](https://www.yanoresearch.com/en/press-release/show/press_id/3921); [PW Consulting, CMP Slurry for Semiconductor Market](https://pmarketresearch.com/worldwide-cmp-slurry-for-semiconductor-market-research/); [PW Consulting, Global CMP Slurries Market](https://pmarketresearch.com/it/cmp-slurries-market/)). A separate, more conservative account-based sensitivity uses **$0.5–$1.2 million per annual account-slot** and **$0.15 million per paid evaluation**, producing a **2028 base case of about $1.425 million** and a **$0.25–$4.2 million range**; the difference reflects model assumptions and definitions, not observed KCTech deal data. These estimates should be revised when product-level prices, customer qualifications, and recurring U.S. sales are known.

## Intel is a nearby anchor, but incumbents already have local strength

Intel Oregon is the clearest nearby target: Oregon’s regulator identifies Intel’s Hillsboro/Ronler Acres and Aloha sites as semiconductor manufacturing facilities, and Intel identifies Oregon as a major research, process-development, and manufacturing location ([Oregon DEQ—Intel Oregon facilities](https://www.oregon.gov/deq/aq/cao/nwr/pages/intel-aloha.aspx); [Intel U.S. manufacturing](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/corporate/usa-chipmaking.html)). Intel reported high-volume manufacturing on Intel 18A at Oregon and Arizona fabs by the end of 2025, which makes the region commercially relevant but does not establish a KCTech evaluation or purchase ([Intel 2025–26 Corporate Responsibility Report](https://www.intel.com.br/content/dam/www/central-libraries/us/en/documents/2026-08/csr-2025-26-full-report.pdf)). Other prospects include Intel Arizona, TSMC Arizona, Microchip’s Gresham fab, GlobalFoundries’ Malta site, and national targets among Micron, Texas Instruments, and Samsung; public sources do not provide comparable site-level slurry purchasing data ([TSMC Arizona](https://www.tsmc.com/static/abouttsmcaz/index.htm?trk=public_post_comment-text); [Microchip Oregon](https://www.microchip.com/en-us/about/careers/microchip-around-the-world/americas/oregon-us); [SIA U.S. Semiconductor Ecosystem Map](https://www.semiconductors.org/ecosystem/)). GlobalFoundries reports **400,000 wafers of annual capacity at Malta**, equivalent to about **33,300 wafers per month**, but wafer capacity is not a slurry-spend estimate ([GlobalFoundries: At a Glance](https://gf.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/GF-At-a-Glance-Jan2026.pdf)). TSMC’s **more than 17 million 12-inch-equivalent wafers of 2025 annual capacity** is company-wide, not U.S.-specific, and likewise cannot be converted directly to KCTech demand ([TSMC 2025 annual report](https://investor.tsmc.com/sites/ir/annual-report/2025/2025%20Annual%20Report.E.pdf)).

Intel is a plausible target, but the public evidence does not establish a current U.S. qualification or purchase commitment. A 2025 report described KCTech’s cooperative relationships with Intel and global foundries as joint research on next-generation materials; a 2026 profile says KCTech supplies CMP slurry to Intel and GlobalFoundries but provides no product, volume, contract, or qualification details ([The Bell, October 23, 2025](https://www.thebell.co.kr/front/newsview.asp?code=0405&key=202510231531579400106938); [Who Is? Yang Ho-geun](https://m.chwho.co.kr/BP?command=mobile_view&num=442673)). A 2016 report provides historical evidence of GlobalFoundries slurry supply, not a current U.S. branch award ([ETNews, May 31, 2016](https://m.etnews.com/20160531000126?obj=Tzo4OiJzdGRDbGFzcyI6Mjp7czo3OiJyZWZlcmVyIjtOO3M6NzoiZm9yd2FyZCI7Tjt9)). The Elec has also reported that KCTech was selected to supply CMP equipment and slurry for an Absolics glass-substrate line, but this was attributed to industry sources and not confirmed by KCTech; it is a possible adjacent opportunity, not a verified production contract ([The Elec—Absolics glass-substrate supply report](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=13280)).

Incumbents make qualification and local support essential. AGC manufactures ceria slurry in Hillsboro, and Fujimi manufactures CMP-related polishing materials at Oregon sites in Tualatin and Wilsonville ([AGC Electronics America—CMP slurry](https://www.agcem.com/products/cmp-slurry/); [Fujimi Corporation—company information](https://www.fujimico.com/about/corporate-information/)). Fujifilm reports **21% global slurry share**, while TECHCET has estimated **31%**; the sources do not reconcile the difference, and neither figure is a U.S.-specific share ([Fujifilm market-position presentation](https://ir.fujifilm.com/en/investors/ir-materials/presentations/session/main/0118/teaserItems1/0/tableContents/01112/multiFileUpload2_1/link/ff_presentation_20250421_002e.pdf); [TECHCET CMP consumables release](https://www.semiconductorpackagingnews.com/uploads/1/Final_CMP_PR_050124SB.pdf)). Fujifilm’s planned **$18 million** Mesa expansion, scheduled to start operating in **2027**, adds another U.S. competitor with local manufacturing plans ([Fujifilm U.S. production expansion](https://newsroom-us.fujifilm.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/Fujifilm-to-Expand-CMP-Slurry-Production-Capacity-in-the-United-States/default.aspx)). Entegris, DuPont/Qnity, Fujimi, Merck, and Resonac also compete across slurry chemistries; commercial estimates place top-five supplier shares at **60%–80%** in North America or the United States, but these figures are not independently verified and should be treated as directional ([IndexBox, North American CMP Slurries](https://www.indexbox.io/store/northern-america-cmp-slurries-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/); [IndexBox, U.S. CMP Slurries](https://www.indexbox.io/store/united-states-cmp-slurries-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)).

## Spend selectively on samples, qualification, and local credibility

The conventional acquisition path is technical and staged: identify a specific process need, approach the appropriate engineering and materials contacts, provide a documentation package, obtain approval for R&D testing, send controlled samples, support trials, and then pursue process-specific production approval. Intel’s published guidance separates limited R&D testing from HVM transfer, which requires further review and material-risk assessment; a historical CMP supplier filing says qualification can take **six months or longer**, while an end-to-end **12–36-month** route to recurring sales is a prudent planning assumption rather than an Intel commitment ([Intel Chemical Material Suppliers EHS Expectations](https://cdrdv2-public.intel.com/814967/material-ehs-supplier-expectations-training.pdf); [SEC-filed CMP supplier disclosure](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1102934/000110293414000027/form10k.htm)). Intel’s Supplier Intake Tool can create visibility with buyers, but registration is not qualification or a promise of evaluation ([Intel, Information for Potential Suppliers](https://www.intel.com/content/dam/www/central-libraries/us/en/documents/information-for-potential-suppliers.pdf)). Personal connections are most useful for reaching the right technical owner and understanding a real process problem; they do not substitute for formal supplier review or process data.

A less conventional route is to use regional research infrastructure to generate application evidence before expensive fab trials. Oregon State University offers external access to materials characterization, cleanroom, and prototyping facilities; its MaSC access charge is **$400 per researcher per month**, plus a **$510 one-time orientation fee per researcher**, with some equipment charged separately ([Oregon State University MaSC fees](https://research.engr.oregonstate.edu/masc/become-user)). OSU’s ATAMI facility publishes external tool rates of **$52, $82, and $114 per hour** for different equipment classes, while the University of Oregon Knight Campus Cleanroom offers external access with pricing on request ([OSU ATAMI rates](https://atami.oregonstate.edu/main/usage-and-service-rates); [University of Oregon Knight Campus Cleanroom](https://knightcampus.uoregon.edu/core-facilities/knight-campus-cleanroom)). These resources can support screening and characterization, but cannot replace a fab’s proprietary CMP tools, recipes, wafers, or HVM qualification. A focused co-development proposal around a measurable performance or supply issue, or a second-source offer, is more credible than a broad portfolio pitch.

For the proposed four-person team, annual operating costs are estimated at **$1.0–$2.1 million before product COGS**, with an additional **$0.25–$0.75 million** for import-led commercial-launch setup; these are planning allowances, not KCTech budgets or vendor quotes. The payroll estimate is anchored to Portland-area occupational wage data and includes an assumed **25%–35%** employer-cost load; shared labs, testing, logistics, travel, compliance, and marketing account for the remainder ([BLS Portland-area occupational employment and wages, May 2025](https://www.bls.gov/regions/west/news-release/2026/occupationalemploymentandwages_portlandor_20260630.htm); [BLS national occupational employment and wages, May 2025](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/pdf/ocwage.pdf)). Budget an additional **$0.3–$1.0 million** as an initial inventory and working-capital placeholder, since product prices, batch sizes, shipping lead times, and customer payment terms are undisclosed. A separate **$150,000–$400,000 annual** allowance for samples, lab analyses, customer trial support, technical travel, and targeted events is a planning estimate; trial-wafer and customer-tool costs may be negotiated or shared.

Importing Korean product is the lowest-capital initial route, but each grade requires a product-specific review of TSCA status, SDS and labeling, transport classification, shelf life, traceability, and import documentation. EPA says TSCA import certification applies to chemical substances and mixtures, including those imported for R&D; new chemicals may require a premanufacture notice at least **90 days** before non-exempt commercial import ([EPA, TSCA requirements for importing chemicals](https://www.epa.gov/tsca-import-export-requirements/tsca-requirements-importing-chemicals)). Hazardous-material transport duties depend on classification and employee functions; PHMSA generally requires recurrent training at least every **three years** for covered employees ([PHMSA, Hazardous Materials Training Requirements](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/about-phmsa/hazardous-materials-training-requirements)). U.S. toll manufacturing might improve response times without a KCTech-built plant, but no qualified partner for KCTech’s formulas is established; local production should wait for credible customer volume because the **$18 million** Fujifilm figure is an existing-site expansion, not a KCTech plant-cost estimate ([Fujifilm U.S. production expansion](https://newsroom-us.fujifilm.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/Fujifilm-to-Expand-CMP-Slurry-Production-Capacity-in-the-United-States/default.aspx)).

## Conclusion

The branch’s strongest path is to remain asset-light while proving one or two KCTech formulations at specific customer processes. Hillsboro provides proximity to Intel and a regional semiconductor ecosystem, but AGC and Fujimi already offer local competition, and Intel should be pursued alongside other national prospects rather than treated as a guaranteed anchor.

Revenue expectations should remain conditional on qualification milestones, not market size alone. Prioritize product readiness and regulatory documentation, secure technical evaluations, and convert those into repeatable, customer-approved supply before committing to tolling or domestic manufacturing; keep funding and staffing aligned with evidence of customer progress.
