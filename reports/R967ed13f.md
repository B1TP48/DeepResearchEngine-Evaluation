# Formal Results Cannot Guarantee Indefinite Alignment Yet

No formal result establishes that an AI system can be guaranteed to keep aligned with human values indefinitely through recursive self-modification, and no result proves the opposite in full generality. The strongest positive results are conditional theorems in idealised models. Everitt et al. show that self-modification is harmless only if the agent's value function anticipates the modification and evaluates the future with its current utility ([Springer, Everitt et al. 2016](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-41649-6_1)). The main negative results are also limited. Löbian obstacles block a naive proof-based agent from trusting its successor, and bounded-rational agents may drift, but neither result applies to learned systems. The deeper problem is that the target itself is not well defined. Observed behaviour does not uniquely identify a reward function without normative assumptions ([Occam's Razor Is Insufficient, NeurIPS 2018](https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2018/armstrong2018neurips-occam/)), and no source gives a formal, machine-checkable definition of coherent extrapolated volition ([MIRI, CEV](http://intelligence.org/files/CEV.pdf)). A question that asks for a proof of indefinite preservation therefore has no proof available. The honest answer is that the claim is unproven in both directions, and that the existing formal tools cover only narrow, idealised cases.

## Self-trust hits Gödelian limits

The most direct obstacle is logical. Löb's theorem states that if a theory at least as strong as Peano Arithmetic proves "if P is provable, then P," then it proves P ([Wikipedia, Löb's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%B6b's_Theorem)). The MIRI tiling-agents work draws the consequence that a consistent theory cannot verify that every proof of a formula implies that formula's truth ([MIRI, Tiling Agents for Self-Modifying AI (draft)](http://intelligence.org/files/TilingAgentsDraft.pdf)). This makes it hard for an agent to trust future selves to be safe without predicting their actions in detail ([Alignment Forum, Vingean Reflection: Open Problems](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/5bd75cc58225bf0670374f9d/vingean-reflection-open-problems)). Tarski's theorem adds that a consistent arithmetic-capable system cannot define its own truth predicate in its own language ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/goedel-incompleteness/)).

These limits are real but bounded in scope. The tiling papers present several workarounds that sustain trust across indefinite sequences of agents, though one of them, the Waterfall, still requires each agent to modify itself at every step ([Alignment Forum](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/5bd75cc58225bf0670374f9d/vingean-reflection-open-problems)). The Waterfall also falls to a procrastination paradox, because it trusts that its future self will achieve the goal and so keeps postponing it ([Intelligent Agent Foundations Forum](https://agentfoundations.org/item?id=320)). MIRI's own guide reports that many solutions to the Löbian obstacle run into unsoundness that comes from too much self-trust ([MIRI Research Guide](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/6vXArgf9NsYxRYkpS/miri-research-guide)). The theorems therefore rule out one naive form of self-verification. They do not establish that value preservation is impossible, and the workarounds sit on a trade-off that remains unresolved.

## Preservation holds only under idealised assumptions

The clearest preservation result is Everitt et al.'s. Self-modification is harmless if and only if the agent's value function anticipates the consequences of self-modification and uses the current utility function to evaluate the future ([Springer, Everitt et al. 2016](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-41649-6_1); [arXiv 1605.03142](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.03142)). For a modification-independent belief and utility, a perfect expected-utility maximiser that resets to its initial policy keeps the same expected discounted utility ([arXiv 2011.06275](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.06275v2)). The same paper also shows the limits. A hedonistic agent with a constant maximal utility self-modifies toward that utility, and an ignorant agent may modify at any time ([arXiv 1605.03142](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.03142)).

The result weakens sharply for realistic agents. A follow-up on bounded-rational agents finds that self-modification may cause exponential performance deterioration and gradual misalignment of a previously aligned agent ([arXiv 2011.06275](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.06275v2)). The authors frame this as a generalisation of the original theorem to bounded-optimisation agents, not a refutation of it. Schmidhuber's Gödel machine has a similar structure. It accepts a self-rewrite only when it can prove the rewrite improves expected utility under its axiomatised utility function ([arXiv cs/0309048](https://arxiv.org/html/cs/0309048v2)), and even an unlimited-resource version must ignore improvements it cannot prove ([Wikipedia, Gödel machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del_machine)). Its guarantee is relative to whatever utility is encoded, so it says nothing about whether that encoding matches human values.

Reward-tampering work is similar in kind. Design principles remove the instrumental incentive to tamper within a causal-influence-diagram model ([Synthese 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-021-03141-4)). Those principles are stated for modelled incentives, not for every self-modification.

## Human values resist formal specification and stable representation

Even if preservation were proven for a fixed utility function, it is unclear what the function would encode. Armstrong and Mindermann show that without normative assumptions, nothing about the human reward function can be deduced from behaviour ([arXiv 1712.05812 via Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Impossibility-of-deducing-preferences-and-from-Armstrong-Mindermann/11ca969b9c4f02bda7ce42f1bdfa491be4beceb1)). A simplicity prior does not fix this, and the simplest hypotheses are generally degenerate ([arXiv 1712.05812](https://arxiv.org/html/1712.05812.pdf)). CEV is an informal, dynamic procedure, and a 2026 position paper argues that its correctness criterion requires solving the whole extrapolation problem ([arXiv 2606.13755](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13755)). Social-choice results bear on this as well. Eckersley's analysis argues that if a learned network's output is a utility function or total ordering, the impossibility problems persist throughout training, although Arrow's theorem may not bind AI systems that are not incentive-compatible ([arXiv 1901.00064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.00064)). A 2026 paper states that value learning is not a strict impossibility theorem but supports the view that proxy optimisation is structurally brittle ([Springer, The embodied ethics alignment problem](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44163-026-01905-w)). A blog post asserts that universal alignment is undecidable, but it cites results I could not locate, so that claim is unverified ([Pentera blog](https://pentera.io/resources/research/what-ai-cannot-decide-for-us/)).

Drift in learned representations has been documented empirically and in formal toy models. Goal misgeneralisation means a system's capabilities generalise while its goal does not, so it competently pursues the wrong objective ([arXiv 2310.18244](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.18244); [arXiv 2105.14111](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.14111v7)). A catastrophic Goodhart result shows that, for some proxies, true value goes to zero as a proxy is optimised harder ([arXiv 2407.14503](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.14503)). A 2026 paper argues that the strong Goodhart regime is a plausible default for general-purpose AI under continued optimisation ([arXiv 2510.02840](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02840)). These are different mechanisms from the Everitt result. They concern the gap between a learned or proxy target and the intended one, not the stability of a fixed utility under self-rewrite. None of the sources quantifies this drift in frontier systems.

## Verification tools and corrigibility fall short of the question

| Approach | What it establishes | Key limit |
|---|---|---|
| Gödel machine ([arXiv cs/0309048](https://arxiv.org/html/cs/0309048v2)) | Rewrites accepted only after a proof of utility gain | Guarantee is relative to the encoded utility |
| Proof-carrying code ([Appel, FPCC](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~appel/papers/fpcc.pdf)) | Machine-checked proof that a fixed artifact meets a safety policy | Does not address repeated self-generated code |
| Guaranteed Safe AI ([Dalrymple et al.](https://deeplearn.org/arxiv/485332/towards-guaranteed-safe-ai:-a-framework-for-ensuring-robust-and-reliable-ai-systems)) | Framework of world model, specification and verifier | A research strategy; no demonstrated result |
| Safe interruptibility ([MIRI summary of Orseau and Armstrong](https://intelligence.org/2016/06/01/new-paper-safely-interruptible-agents/)) | Some standard RL agents satisfy interruptibility | Concerns policy learning, not self-rewrite of the learner |
| Corrigibility ([LessWrong digest](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5bd75cc58225bf0670374f04/forum-digest-corrigibility-utility-indifference-and-related-control-ideas)) | Analysis of toy models | None of the proposals meets all desiderata, per a secondary summary |

Proof-carrying code checks a single artifact against a safety policy, and extending it to a chain of self-generated code would need a separate soundness argument ([Appel, FPCC](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~appel/papers/fpcc.pdf)). Empirical self-modifying systems such as the Darwin Gödel Machine replace proofs with benchmark evaluation. A 2026 preprint notes that their gains do not by themselves establish safety ([arXiv 2607.12254](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12254)). Recent preprints on recursive self-improvement restrict their existence proofs to a finite simulation horizon ([arXiv 2607.04277](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.04277)). These preprints are unreviewed.

Leading surveys list the problems as open. A 2025 ACM survey names inferring human preferences from behaviour and handling changing or influenceable human values as significant open problems ([ACM Computing Surveys](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3770749)). The same survey identifies goal misgeneralisation, adversarial distribution shift and deceptive alignment as threats to maintaining alignment ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3770749)). None of the overviews I could access claims a proof of indefinite preservation.

## Conclusion

The question as posed cannot be answered yes, and the evidence does not support a proof that it is impossible. What can be proven are conditional statements: a fixed utility function that an agent evaluates with its current utility is preserved through self-modification under perfect rationality and modification-independence ([Springer, Everitt et al. 2016](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-41649-6_1)). Relaxing those assumptions to bounded agents removes the guarantee and allows misalignment ([arXiv 2011.06275](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.06275v2)). The Löbian and Gödelian results constrain naive self-verification, and the tiling workarounds carry trade-offs that remain open ([MIRI draft](http://intelligence.org/files/TilingAgentsDraft.pdf)).

The most important gap lies upstream of any self-modification proof. A guarantee of "alignment with human values" is well-posed only once the target is fixed as a function, distribution, ordering or extrapolation procedure, and each choice faces its own identifiability or social-choice constraints ([Occam's Razor Is Insufficient, NeurIPS 2018](https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2018/armstrong2018neurips-occam/)). Anyone who claims a guarantee should state the target, the rationality assumptions, the horizon and the self-evaluation rule, and check each one. Work on verification and corrigibility should aim at narrower, testable claims about specific modification classes, not at an unbounded guarantee that current formal results cannot support.
